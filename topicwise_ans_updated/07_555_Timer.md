## 1. Internal Architecture & Pin Configuration
*[Appeared in: 2024 Q6(c), 2022 Q4(a), 2021 Q8(a), 2019 Q7(a), 2018 Q7(a), 2017 Q7(a), 2017 Q7(c)]*

**Question:**
* **(a)** Explain the internal operational blocks of a 555 timer IC with its complete block diagram. **[04 Marks, CLO1]**
* **(c)** Explain the functional role of each of the 8 pins in a standard 555 timer IC. **[03 Marks, CO2]**

**Answer:**
### 1.1 Standard 8-Pin Configuration
**Pinout Diagram:**
```text
        +---\/---+
    GND |1      8| VCC
   TRIG |2  555 7| DIS
    OUT |3      6| THR
    RST |4      5| CV
        +--------+
```
*   **Pin 1 (GND):** System ground reference.
*   **Pin 2 (TRIG):** Trigger input. When the voltage drops below $1/3 V_{CC}$, the output (Pin 3) is driven HIGH.
*   **Pin 3 (OUT):** Output terminal, capable of sinking or sourcing up to $200\text{ mA}$ to drive external loads.
*   **Pin 4 (RST):** Reset input (active LOW). Ties directly to $V_{CC}$ if unused to prevent accidental resets.
*   **Pin 5 (CV):** Control Voltage. It connects to the internal $2/3 V_{CC}$ divider node. You can bypass it to ground with a $0.01\ \mu\text{F}$ capacitor for noise immunity. You can also drive it externally to change thresholds.
*   **Pin 6 (THR):** Threshold input. When the voltage rises above $2/3 V_{CC}$, the output is forced LOW and the discharge transistor turns ON.
*   **Pin 7 (DIS):** Discharge pin. Connected internally to the open collector of an NPN transistor. It grounds the external timing capacitor when the output is LOW.
*   **Pin 8 (VCC):** Positive supply rail ($+4.5\text{ V}$ to $+15\text{ V}$).

### 1.2 Internal Functional Blocks
**Functional Block Diagram:**
```text
               VCC (8)
                |
               [R] 5k
                |
                +-----> (5) Control Voltage
                |
              [ | ] Upper Comparator
   THR (6) o--[>| ]-----+
                        |    R   S Flip-Flop
               [R] 5k   +---(S   Q)--------+----|>o----o (3) OUT
                |           (     )        |   Buffer
              [ | ] Lower +-(R  Q')--+     |
   TRIG (2) o-[>| ]-------+          |     |
                |                    |     |
               [R] 5k                |   |/
                |          RST (4) o-+---|  Discharge Transistor
               GND (1)                   |>
                                           |
                                           +---------o (7) DIS
                                           |
                                          GND (1)
```
1.  **Voltage Divider:** Three matched $5\text{ k}\Omega$ resistors in series from $V_{CC}$ to Ground. This gives the "555" its name. It creates internal references exactly at $2/3 V_{CC}$ and $1/3 V_{CC}$.
2.  **Dual Comparators:**
    *   **Upper (Threshold) Comparator:** Compares the external voltage at Pin 6 against the $2/3 V_{CC}$ reference.
    *   **Lower (Trigger) Comparator:** Compares the external voltage at Pin 2 against the $1/3 V_{CC}$ reference.
3.  **RS Flip-Flop:** A bi-stable memory element driven by the two comparators. It stores the timer's current logical state.
4.  **Discharge Transistor:** An internal NPN transistor. The inverted Flip-Flop output drives its base. When the timer output goes LOW, this transistor turns ON. This shorts Pin 7 to ground.

## 2. Astable Multivibrator Mode

### 2.1 Working Principles & Equations
*[Appeared in: 2024 Q6(a), 2023 Q7(a), 2021 Q4(b), 2020 Q8(a)]*

**Question:**
* **(a)** Explain the step-by-step working principles of a 555-timer based astable multivibrator. **[04/05 Marks, CO2]**
* **(b)** Formulate the equations to determine the charging and discharging time periods of the timing capacitor in a 555-timer astable multivibrator. **[04 Marks, CLO2]**

**Answer:**
In Astable mode, Pins 2 and 6 are tied together. This lets the circuit trigger itself continuously. It acts as a free-running square wave oscillator.
```text
                      +Vcc
                       |
                      [ ] RA
                       |
                       +------- Pin 7 (Discharge)
                       |
                      [ ] RB
                       |
                       +------- Pin 6 (Threshold) & Pin 2 (Trigger)
                       |
                     +---+ C
                     | | |
                     +---+
                       |
                      Gnd
```

**Step-by-Step Operation & Timing Formulations:**
1.  **Charging Phase ($t_H$):** Upon power-up, the output goes HIGH. The internal discharge transistor (Pin 7) is OFF. The external capacitor $C$ charges from $1/3 V_{CC}$ toward $V_{CC}$ through $R_A$ and $R_B$.
    *   *Equation:* The time required to charge from $1/3 V_{CC}$ to $2/3 V_{CC}$ is mathematically derived as $t_H = \ln(2) (R_A + R_B) C \approx 0.693 (R_A + R_B) C$.
2.  **Discharging Phase ($t_L$):** The capacitor voltage hits $2/3 V_{CC}$. The upper comparator trips and resets the flip-flop. The output goes LOW. The discharge transistor turns ON, grounding Pin 7. The capacitor discharges from $2/3 V_{CC}$ down to $1/3 V_{CC}$ through $R_B$.
    *   *Equation:* The time required to discharge from $2/3 V_{CC}$ to $1/3 V_{CC}$ is $t_L = \ln(2) R_B C \approx 0.693 R_B C$.
3.  **Total Period & Frequency:**
    *   $T = t_H + t_L = 0.693 (R_A + 2 R_B) C$
    *   $f_o = \frac{1}{T} \approx \frac{1.44}{(R_A + 2 R_B) C}$
4.  **Duty Cycle ($D$):** The ratio of HIGH time to total time.
    *   $D = \frac{t_H}{T} = \frac{R_A + R_B}{R_A + 2 R_B} \times 100\%$
    *(Note: Without a bypass diode, the duty cycle is always strictly $>50\%$.)*

### 2.2 Astable Oscillator Numericals
*[Appeared in: 2019 Q7(c), 2017 Q7(b), 2019 Q8(c)]*

**Question 1 (Design for specific frequency and duty cycle):**
* Design a square wave generator using a 555 timer IC. Assume $V_{CC} = 12\text{ V}$, $f_o = 3.5\text{ kHz}$, and duty cycle $D = 60\%$. **[05 Marks]**

**Answer 1:**
1.  **Find resistor ratio from duty cycle:** $D = \frac{R_A + R_B}{R_A + 2 R_B} = 0.60 \Rightarrow 0.4 R_A = 0.2 R_B \Rightarrow R_B = 2 R_A$.
2.  **Find components from frequency:** $f_o = \frac{1.44}{(R_A + 2 R_B) C} = 3500\text{ Hz}$.
3.  Substitute $R_B = 2 R_A$: $3500 = \frac{1.44}{5 R_A C} \Rightarrow R_A C \approx 8.228 \times 10^{-5}$.
4.  Choose standard $C = 10\text{ nF}$. Then $R_A = 8.2\text{ k}\Omega$ and $R_B = 16.4\text{ k}\Omega$.

**Question 2 (Design for specific $t_H$ and $t_L$):**
* Find the timing values and design the resistor values ($R_A, R_B$) of an astable network given $C = 0.01\ \mu\text{F}$, $t_H = 20\ \mu\text{s}$, and $t_L = 10\ \mu\text{s}$. **[06 Marks]**

**Answer 2:**
1.  **Discharge Phase ($t_L$):** $t_L = 0.693 R_B C \Rightarrow 10\ \mu\text{s} = 0.693 R_B (0.01\ \mu\text{F})$.
    $R_B = \frac{10 \times 10^{-6}}{0.693 \times 0.01 \times 10^{-6}} = \frac{1000}{0.693} \approx 1443\ \Omega = 1.44\text{ k}\Omega$.
2.  **Charging Phase ($t_H$):** $t_H = 0.693 (R_A + R_B) C \Rightarrow 20\ \mu\text{s} = 0.693 (R_A + 1443) (0.01\ \mu\text{F})$.
    $R_A + 1443 = \frac{2000}{0.693} \approx 2886\ \Omega \Rightarrow R_A = 2886 - 1443 = 1443\ \Omega = 1.44\text{ k}\Omega$.

**Question 3 (Calculate frequency from components):**
* Calculate the free-running frequency of an astable multivibrator with $R_A = 4\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_B = 4\text{ k}\Omega$, and $C = 0.01\ \mu\text{F}$. **[04 Marks]**

**Answer 3:**
1.  **Apply frequency formula:**
    $$ f_o = \frac{1.44}{(R_A + 2 R_B) C} = \frac{1.44}{(4000 + 8000)(0.01 \times 10^{-6})} = \frac{1.44}{12000 \times 10^{-8}} = \frac{1.44}{1.2 \times 10^{-4}} = 12,000\text{ Hz} = 12\text{ kHz} $$

### 2.3 Astable Mode with Bypass Diode (Duty Cycle $\le 50\%$)
*[Appeared in: 2018 Q7(b)]*

**Question:**
* **(b)** Define the term duty cycle. Calculate the duty cycle of the 555-timer astable multivibrator network shown below. The circuit includes an ideal diode across the discharge resistor $R_B$. **[05 Marks]**

**Answer:**
**Definition:** Duty cycle is the percentage of the total period during which the output signal is in the active HIGH state.

**Diode Bypass Operation:**
To get a duty cycle of $50\%$ or less, put a diode $D_1$ in parallel across $R_B$ (anode at Pin 7, cathode at Pin 6/2).
1.  **Charging Phase ($t_H$):** Current flows from $V_{CC}$ through $R_A$. Diode $D_1$ turns ON and bypasses $R_B$. The capacitor charges *only* through $R_A$.
    $$ t_H \approx 0.693 R_A C $$
2.  **Discharging Phase ($t_L$):** Current discharges into Pin 7. The diode is reverse-biased (blocking). The capacitor must discharge through $R_B$.
    $$ t_L = 0.693 R_B C $$
3.  **New Duty Cycle:**
    $$ D = \frac{t_H}{t_H + t_L} = \frac{R_A}{R_A + R_B} \times 100\% $$

**Numerical Calculation (from circuit provided in 2018 Q7(b)):**
Given $R_A = 1\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_B = 10\text{ k}\Omega$, $C = 0.1\ \mu\text{F}$.
$$ D = \frac{1\text{ k}\Omega}{1\text{ k}\Omega + 10\text{ k}\Omega} \times 100\% = \frac{1}{11} \times 100\% \approx 9.09\% $$

## 3. Monostable Multivibrator Mode

### 3.1 Operating States and Pulse Width Derivation
*[Appeared in: 2022 Q7(a), 2020 Q8(a), 2020 Q6(a)]*

**Question:**
* **(a)** Show that the output pulse width ($W$) of a 555-timer monostable multivibrator is given by $W \approx 1.1 R C$. **[04 Marks]**

**Answer:**
A monostable multivibrator is a "one-shot" timer. It rests in a LOW state. It needs a negative trigger pulse at Pin 2 to switch HIGH. It stays HIGH for a set time ($W$). Then it automatically returns to LOW.

**Circuit Operation:**
*   **Stable State:** Output is LOW. The internal discharge transistor is ON. This holds the external capacitor $C$ at $0\text{V}$.
*   **Triggered State:** A negative pulse at Pin 2 sets the flip-flop. The output goes HIGH. The discharge transistor turns OFF. Capacitor $C$ charges from $0\text{V}$ toward $V_{CC}$ through resistor $R$.
*   **Return to Stable:** Capacitor voltage reaches $2/3 V_{CC}$. The upper comparator resets the flip-flop. The output drops LOW. The discharge transistor turns ON. It rapidly discharges $C$ back to $0\text{V}$.

**Pulse Width Derivation:**
The standard capacitor charging equation is:
$$ v_c(t) = V_{final} - (V_{final} - V_{initial}) e^{-t / RC} $$
Here, $V_{initial} = 0\text{V}$ and $V_{final} = V_{CC}$. The threshold point is $v_c(W) = \frac{2}{3} V_{CC}$.
$$ \frac{2}{3} V_{CC} = V_{CC} - (V_{CC} - 0) e^{-W / RC} $$
Divide by $V_{CC}$:
$$ \frac{2}{3} = 1 - e^{-W / RC} \Rightarrow e^{-W / RC} = \frac{1}{3} $$
Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
$$ -\frac{W}{RC} = \ln \left( \frac{1}{3} \right) \approx -1.0986 $$
$$ W \approx 1.1 RC $$

### 3.2 Monostable Output Waveform Sketch
*[Appeared in: 2018 Q7(c)]*

**Question:**
* **(c)** Sketch the output voltage waveform ($V_o$) of a 555-timer monostable multivibrator triggered with a negative-going pulse at $t = 1\text{ ms}$ and $t = 5\text{ ms}$. Given $R = 4.7\text{ k}\Omega$ and $C = 0.1\ \mu\text{F}$. **[04 Marks]**

**Answer:**
**Step 1: Calculate the standard pulse width ($W$)**
$$ W = 1.1 R C = 1.1 \times 4.7\text{ k}\Omega \times 0.1\ \mu\text{F} = 0.517\text{ ms} $$

**Step 2: Analyze the trigger events**
*   **At $t = 1\text{ ms}$:** The first trigger arrives. The output goes HIGH immediately. It stays HIGH for exactly $0.517\text{ ms}$. So it falls back LOW at $t = 1.517\text{ ms}$.
*   **At $t = 5\text{ ms}$:** The second trigger arrives. The output goes HIGH again. It stays HIGH for exactly $0.517\text{ ms}$. So it falls back LOW at $t = 5.517\text{ ms}$.

**Step 3: Sketch the Waveform**
The output $V_o(t)$ rests at $0\text{V}$. At $t=1\text{ms}$, it jumps to $+V_{CC}$ and forms a rectangular pulse ending at $1.517\text{ms}$. It remains $0\text{V}$ until $t=5\text{ms}$, where it forms an identical pulse ending at $5.517\text{ms}$.

## 4. Specialized 555 Applications

### 4.1 Frequency Divider
*[Appeared in: 2019 Q7(b)]*

**Question:**
* **(b)** "A monostable multivibrator can be used as a frequency divider." Justify this statement with timing diagrams and circuit explanations. **[04 Marks]**

**Answer:**
**Justification:** A monostable multivibrator ignores triggers while it is HIGH. You can tune the $RC$ time constant. You set the pulse width $W$ to be slightly longer than $N$ cycles of the input.
*   **Operation:** Set $W$ to span two input pulses. The 555 timer triggers on the 1st pulse. It ignores the 2nd pulse because it is already HIGH. It returns to LOW before the 3rd pulse. Then it triggers again on the 3rd pulse.
*   **Result:** The output frequency is exactly $f_{out} = f_{in} / 2$. By adjusting $W$, it can divide by any integer $N$.

### 4.2 Missing Pulse Detector
*[Appeared in: 2021 Q8(c), 2020 Q6(c)]*

**Question:**
* **(c)** Draw the circuit diagram and explain the operation of a 555-timer based missing pulse detector circuit. **[04 Marks]**

**Answer:**
**Operation:**
A missing pulse detector watches a continuous train of input pulses. If a pulse fails to arrive within the set time window, the circuit triggers an alarm.
1.  **Configuration:** It uses a monostable multivibrator. A PNP transistor connects in parallel with capacitor $C$. The incoming pulses drive the PNP base.
2.  **Normal Condition:** Incoming pulses rapidly turn ON the PNP transistor before capacitor $C$ charges to $2/3 V_{CC}$. Each pulse discharges the capacitor to $0\text{V}$. This resets the timing cycle. The output stays HIGH.
3.  **Missing Pulse Event:** The input pulses suddenly stop. The PNP transistor stays OFF. Capacitor $C$ charges without interruption until it hits $2/3 V_{CC}$. The 555 timer finishes its monostable cycle. The output goes LOW. This flags the missing pulse.

### 4.3 FSK Modulator
*[Appeared in: 2019 Q8(a)]*

**Question:**
* **(a)** Explain how a 555 timer IC can be configured as a Frequency Shift Keying (FSK) modulator. **[04 Marks]**

**Answer:**
**Configuration:** A 555 timer configured as an Astable oscillator generates a steady carrier frequency. Pin 5 (Control Voltage) is used to modulate this frequency.
**Operation:**
1.  Pin 5 connects to the internal $2/3 V_{CC}$ node. You apply external digital voltages (Logic 0 or Logic 1) to Pin 5. This forces the upper threshold voltage up or down.
2.  Pin 5 voltage increases (Logic 1). The capacitor takes longer to reach the new threshold. The output frequency shifts **lower**.
3.  Pin 5 voltage decreases (Logic 0). The capacitor reaches the threshold faster. The output frequency shifts **higher**.
4.  This creates a waveform that jumps between two frequencies based on digital inputs. This is Frequency Shift Keying (FSK).
