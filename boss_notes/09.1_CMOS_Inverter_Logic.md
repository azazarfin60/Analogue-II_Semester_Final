# CMOS Logic Gates
> **Topic:** CMOS Logic | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** 5/8 years

## 1. CMOS Technology Principles
*[Appeared in: 2023 Q3(c), 2021 Q8b]*

CMOS (**Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor**) technology pairs complementary p-channel (PMOS) and n-channel (NMOS) enhancement-mode MOSFETs. It is the dominant technology for all modern digital ICs (including computer processors) due to its massive structural advantages:

1. **Ultra-Low Static Power Dissipation:** Under steady-state conditions, either the pull-up network (PMOS) or the pull-down network (NMOS) is entirely OFF. Because they are in series, no direct DC path ever exists between the power supply ($V_{DD}$) and Ground. Static power dissipation is in the nanowatt range. Current is only drawn for a microsecond during active logic transitions.
2. **Symmetrical Rail-to-Rail Swings:** The HIGH logic state is pulled directly to $+V_{DD}$ (via an ON PMOS), and the LOW logic state is pulled directly to Ground (via an ON NMOS). The signal doesn't suffer voltage drops like it does in BJT logic.
3. **High Input Impedance:** Because the MOS gate is insulated by a thin layer of silicon dioxide ($SiO_2$), CMOS inputs draw virtually zero DC gate leakage current.
4. **High Packing Density:** MOSFETs are physically microscopic compared to BJTs and require no internal isolation resistors, allowing billions of gates to be fabricated on a single CPU die.

## 2. The CMOS Inverter (NOT Gate)
*[Appeared in: 2017 Q1(c)]*

This is the fundamental building block.
* **Pull-up:** One PMOS (Source to $V_{DD}$, Drain to Output).
* **Pull-down:** One NMOS (Source to Ground, Drain to Output).
* **Inputs:** The two gates are tied together to form $V_{in}$.

```
                      +VDD
                       |
                     [ PMOS ] 
                       |
        Vin o----------+---> Vout
                       |
                     [ NMOS ] 
                       |
                      Gnd
```

### Operation:
- **Input HIGH ($V_{DD}$):** NMOS turns ON ($V_{GS} = V_{DD}$). PMOS turns OFF ($V_{GS} = 0$). Output is pulled to **Ground (LOW)**.
- **Input LOW ($0\text{V}$):** NMOS turns OFF ($V_{GS} = 0$). PMOS turns ON ($V_{GS} = -V_{DD}$). Output is pulled to **$V_{DD}$ (HIGH)**.

## 3. Designing 2-Input Gates

The golden rule of CMOS design:
- **PMOS (Pull-up network):** ON when input is LOW. Connect in **Parallel** for NAND, **Series** for NOR.
- **NMOS (Pull-down network):** ON when input is HIGH. Connect in **Series** for NAND, **Parallel** for NOR.
*(Notice they are always the exact topological opposite of each other!)*

### 3.1 2-Input NAND Gate
*[Appeared in: 2022 Q4(a), 2021 Q8(c)]*
- **PMOS:** Parallel.
- **NMOS:** Series.
If *both* inputs are HIGH, both series NMOS turn ON, pulling the output LOW. If *any* input is LOW, the series path breaks, and a parallel PMOS turns ON, pulling the output HIGH.

### 3.2 2-Input NOR Gate
*[Appeared in: 2021 Q8(c)]*
- **PMOS:** Series.
- **NMOS:** Parallel.
If *both* inputs are LOW, both series PMOS turn ON, pulling the output HIGH. If *any* input is HIGH, the series path breaks, and a parallel NMOS turns ON, pulling the output LOW.

## 4. Designing a 3-Input NAND Gate
*[Appeared in: 2022 Q1(c), 2017 Q2(a)]*

This is the most common 6-mark drawing/truth-table question.
To implement $V_{out} = \overline{A \cdot B \cdot C}$:
- **Pull-up Network:** Three PMOS transistors ($Q_{P1}, Q_{P2}, Q_{P3}$) connected in **parallel** between $+V_{DD}$ and the output node.
- **Pull-down Network:** Three NMOS transistors ($Q_{N1}, Q_{N2}, Q_{N3}$) connected in **series** between the output node and Ground.

```
                           +VDD
                 +-----+-----+-----+
                 |     |     |     |
                Qp1   Qp2   Qp3    |   (Parallel PMOS Pull-up)
                 |     |     |     |
                 +-----+-----+-----+---> Vout
                       |
                      Qn1
                       |
                      Qn2              (Series NMOS Pull-down)
                       |
                      Qn3
                       |
                      Gnd
```

### Truth Table Analysis

| Input A | Input B | Input C | $Q_{P1}$ | $Q_{P2}$ | $Q_{P3}$ | $Q_{N1}$ | $Q_{N2}$ | $Q_{N3}$ | Output $V_{out}$ |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **0** | **0** | **0** | ON | ON | ON | OFF | OFF | OFF | **1** |
| **0** | **0** | **1** | ON | ON | OFF | OFF | OFF | ON | **1** |
| **0** | **1** | **0** | ON | OFF | ON | OFF | ON | OFF | **1** |
| **0** | **1** | **1** | ON | OFF | OFF | OFF | ON | ON | **1** |
| **1** | **0** | **0** | OFF | ON | ON | ON | OFF | OFF | **1** |
| **1** | **0** | **1** | OFF | ON | OFF | ON | OFF | ON | **1** |
| **1** | **1** | **0** | OFF | OFF | ON | ON | ON | OFF | **1** |
| **1** | **1** | **1** | OFF | OFF | OFF | ON | ON | ON | **0** |

**Verification:** The output is LOW (0) *only* when all three inputs are HIGH (1) turning all three series NMOS transistors ON. In all other cases, the series path is broken and at least one PMOS pulls the output HIGH (1). This perfectly matches NAND logic.

## ⚡ Exam Tips
- The phrase **"Ultra-Low Static Power Dissipation"** is the most important feature of CMOS. Never omit it.
- In drawing questions, clearly label the PMOS transistors with a small circle (bubble) at the gate terminal to distinguish them from the NMOS transistors.
- When generating a truth table for complex CMOS gates, fill out the transistor states first (0 turns PMOS ON, 1 turns NMOS ON). The output logic follows naturally.
