# Mathematical Circuit Design
> **Topic:** Op-Amp Applications | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** 6/8 years

## Designing Analog Computers

One of the most common 6-mark questions on the exam is designing an op-amp circuit to solve a specific mathematical equation. You are given an equation like $V_o = 3V_1 - 2V_2 + 5 \int V_3 dt$ and asked to draw the schematic.

**The Golden Rules of Circuit Design:**
1. **The Inverting Summer is King:** All final additions and subtractions should be done using a single *Inverting Summing Amplifier* at the very end of your circuit.

   $$
   V_o = -\left( \frac{R_f}{R_1}V_1 + \frac{R_f}{R_2}V_2 \dots \right)
   $$

2. **Watch Your Signs:** Because the summer *inverts* everything, if the final equation wants a $+3V_1$, you must feed $-3V_1$ into the summer. If it wants $-2V_2$, you must feed $+2V_2$ into the summer.
3. **Pre-Processing Stages:** Use dedicated op-amp stages (inverting amps, non-inverting amps, differentiators) to prepare each signal to the correct sign and magnitude *before* feeding them all into the final summer.
4. **Assume Reasonable Values:** Unless specified, always choose standard resistor values like $10\text{ k}\Omega$ or $100\text{ k}\Omega$, and capacitor values like $1\ \mu\text{F}$ or $10\ \mu\text{F}$.

## Worked Example 1: Differentiator + Summing
*[Appeared in: 2019 Q4(a), 2018 Q4(b)]*

**Design a circuit to implement:**

$$
v_o = 0.5 v_1 - 0.7 v_2 + 0.2 \frac{d v_3}{d t}
$$

### Step 1: Analyze the Final Summer
We will use an inverting summer at the end. It performs the operation $v_o = -(V_A + V_B + V_C)$.
To get the desired $v_o$, we must feed it the *negatives* of what we want:
- We want $+0.5 v_1$, so we must feed it $-0.5 v_1$
- We want $-0.7 v_2$, so we must feed it $+0.7 v_2$ (Wait, if we feed it $v_2$ directly, we can just use the summer's own gain to get $-0.7v_2$. Let's look closer).

Actually, the easiest way to design this is to set the final summer's feedback resistor $R_f = 100\text{ k}\Omega$, and let the summer do the final scaling!
Let's define the final summer equation:

$$
v_o = -\left( \frac{R_f}{R_a}v_a + \frac{R_f}{R_b}v_b + \frac{R_f}{R_2}v_2 \right)
$$

### Step 2: Differentiator Channel ($v_3$)
We need a term of $+0.2 \frac{dv_3}{dt}$.
Let's pass $v_3$ through an inverting differentiator. Its output is:

$$
v_a = -R_d C_d \frac{dv_3}{dt}
$$

Let $C_d = 1\ \mu\text{F}$. To get a coefficient of $0.2$, we need $R_d \times 1\mu = 0.2 \implies R_d = 200\text{ k}\Omega$.
So, $v_a = -0.2 \frac{dv_3}{dt}$.
If we feed $v_a$ into the final summer with a gain of $-1$, the final output will be $+0.2 \frac{dv_3}{dt}$. Perfect!
- **Summer Input $R_a$:** To get a gain of $-1$ (since $R_f = 100\text{k}$), we set $R_a = 100\text{ k}\Omega$.

### Step 3: Gain Channel 1 ($v_1$)
We need $+0.5 v_1$.
Let's pass $v_1$ through a standard inverting amplifier to get a negative version.

$$
v_b = -\left(\frac{R_{f1}}{R_{i1}}\right) v_1
$$

Choose $R_{i1} = 100\text{ k}\Omega$ and $R_{f1} = 50\text{ k}\Omega$. This gives $v_b = -0.5 v_1$.
If we feed $v_b$ into the final summer with a gain of $-1$, the output becomes $+0.5 v_1$. Perfect!
- **Summer Input $R_b$:** To get a gain of $-1$, set $R_b = 100\text{ k}\Omega$.

### Step 4: Direct Channel ($v_2$)
We need $-0.7 v_2$.
Since the summer automatically inverts, we can just plug $v_2$ directly into the final summer! We just need the summer channel to have a gain of $-0.7$.

$$
-\frac{R_f}{R_2} = -0.7 \implies \frac{100\text{k}}{R_2} = 0.7 \implies R_2 = \frac{100\text{k}}{0.7} = 142.8\text{ k}\Omega
$$

### Step 5: Final Check

$$
v_o = -\left( \frac{100\text{k}}{100\text{k}}v_a + \frac{100\text{k}}{100\text{k}}v_b + \frac{100\text{k}}{142.8\text{k}}v_2 \right)
$$

$$
v_o = -( v_a + v_b + 0.7v_2 )
$$

Substitute $v_a$ and $v_b$:

$$
v_o = -( -0.2\frac{dv_3}{dt} - 0.5v_1 + 0.7v_2 ) = 0.2\frac{dv_3}{dt} + 0.5v_1 - 0.7v_2
$$

*Matches the required equation exactly.*

## Worked Example 2: Negative and Positive Integrals
*[Appeared in: 2020 Q8(b)]*

**Design a circuit to implement:**

$$
V_o = 3 V_1 - 2 V_2 - 3 \frac{d V_1}{d t}
$$

1. **Gain Channel 1 ($V_1$):** We want $+3V_1$.
   Pass $V_1$ to an inverting stage with gain $-3$ ($R_i = 10\text{ k}\Omega, R_f = 30\text{ k}\Omega$).
   Result: $V_{1a} = -3V_1$.
2. **Gain Channel 2 ($V_2$):** We want $-2V_2$.
   Pass $V_2$ to a non-inverting stage with gain $+2$ ($R_i = 10\text{ k}\Omega, R_f = 10\text{ k}\Omega$).
   Result: $V_{2a} = +2V_2$.
3. **Differentiator Channel ($V_1$):** We want $-3 \frac{dV_1}{dt}$.
   Pass $V_1$ through an inverting differentiator ($R_d = 300\text{ k}\Omega, C_d = 10\ \mu\text{F}$). This yields $-3 \frac{dV_1}{dt}$.
   But wait! The final summer will invert it again, turning it positive! We must pass it through a unity-gain inverter ($R_i=10\text{k}, R_f=10\text{k}$) to flip the sign to $+3 \frac{dV_1}{dt}$.
   Result: $V_{da} = +3 \frac{dV_1}{dt}$.
4. **Final Inverting Summer:**
   Set $R_f = 10\text{ k}\Omega$. Set all input resistors to $10\text{ k}\Omega$ (gain of $-1$).

   $$
   V_o = -(V_{1a} + V_{2a} + V_{da})
   $$

   $$
   V_o = -(-3V_1 + 2V_2 + 3\frac{dV_1}{dt})
   $$

   $$
   V_o = 3V_1 - 2V_2 - 3\frac{dV_1}{dt}
   $$

## ⚡ Exam Tips
- There are infinite correct ways to design these circuits! As long as your math checks out, your resistor values are correct.
- If you get confused by the signs, write the final summer equation out with placeholders ($V_A, V_B, V_C$) and work backwards. It guarantees you won't make a sign error.
- **Draw the schematic clearly.** Use triangular op-amp symbols, label all resistor and capacitor values, and label the intermediate nodes ($V_{1a}, V_{2a}$) so the examiner can follow your logic.
