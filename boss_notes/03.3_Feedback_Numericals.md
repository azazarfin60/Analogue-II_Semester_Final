# Feedback Worked Numericals
> **Topic:** Feedback Amplifiers | **Priority:** 🟡 IMPORTANT | **Exam Frequency:** 3/8 years

## Problem 1: Voltage-Series Parameter Extraction
*[Appeared in: 2018 Q6(b)]*

**Given:** 
A voltage-series feedback amplifier has open-loop parameters: 
- Voltage gain $A = -100$
- Input impedance $R_i = 10\text{ k}\Omega$
- Output impedance $R_o = 20\text{ k}\Omega$
- The feedback factor is $\beta = -0.1$

**Goal:** Calculate the closed-loop parameters ($A_f, R_{if}, R_{of}$).

### Step 1: Calculate the Desensitivity Factor ($D$)
Always calculate this first, as it's the core multiplier/divider for everything else.

$$
D = 1 + A\beta = 1 + (-100)(-0.1) = 1 + 10 = \mathbf{11}
$$

### Step 2: Closed-Loop Voltage Gain ($A_f$)
Gain always goes down by a factor of $D$.

$$
A_f = \frac{A}{D} = \frac{-100}{11} \approx \boxed{ \mathbf{-9.09} }
$$

### Step 3: Closed-Loop Input Impedance ($R_{if}$)
This is a "Series" input connection. Series means the impedance *increases* by a factor of $D$.

$$
R_{if} = R_i \times D = 10\text{ k}\Omega \times 11 = \boxed{ \mathbf{110\text{ k}\Omega} }
$$

### Step 4: Closed-Loop Output Impedance ($R_{of}$)
This is a "Voltage" (parallel/shunt) output sampling connection. Parallel means the impedance *decreases* by a factor of $D$.

$$
R_{of} = \frac{R_o}{D} = \frac{20\text{ k}\Omega}{11} \approx \boxed{ \mathbf{1.818\text{ k}\Omega} }
$$

## Problem 2: JFET CS Feedback Loop Analysis
*[Appeared in: 2017 Q2(b)]*

**Given:** 
A JFET CS amplifier has $g_m = 5800\ \mu\text{S}$. 
It is configured with a feedback resistor divider: $R_1 = 100\text{ k}\Omega$ and $R_2 = 20\text{ k}\Omega$ (where $R_2$ connects across the input to ground, and $R_1$ feeds from output to $R_2$). 
Load components: $R_D = 10\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_L = 10\text{ k}\Omega$. 

**Goal:** Calculate the absolute change in gain magnitude caused by adding the feedback loop.

### Step 1: Gain Without Feedback ($A$)
With the feedback loop "open" (disconnected), the amplifier just sees the drain resistor and the load resistor.
The AC load resistance at the drain is:

$$
R_L' = R_D \parallel R_L = 10\text{ k}\Omega \parallel 10\text{ k}\Omega = 5\text{ k}\Omega
$$

The open-loop gain of a standard CS amplifier is:

$$
A = -g_m R_L' = -(5800 \times 10^{-6}\text{ S}) \times 5000\ \Omega = \mathbf{-29}
$$

### Step 2: The Feedback Factor ($\beta$)
The feedback network is just a simple voltage divider made of $R_1$ and $R_2$. The voltage returned to the source terminal is the voltage across $R_2$:

$$
\beta = -\frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2} = -\frac{20\text{ k}}{100\text{ k} + 20\text{ k}} = -\frac{20}{120} \approx \mathbf{-0.1667}
$$

*(Note: the negative sign indicates it's an inverting feedback loop relative to the input).*

### Step 3: Gain With Feedback ($A_f$)
Now apply the standard feedback formula:

$$
A_f = \frac{A}{1 + A\beta} = \frac{-29}{1 + (-29)(-0.1667)} = \frac{-29}{1 + 4.834} = \frac{-29}{5.834} \approx \mathbf{-4.97}
$$

### Step 4: The Change
The question asked for the *change* in gain magnitude.

- Gain magnitude without feedback: $|-29| = 29$
- Gain magnitude with feedback: $|-4.97| = 4.97$
- Absolute change: $29 - 4.97 = \boxed{ \mathbf{24.03} }$ 

*(The feedback reduced the gain by an enormous 82.8%!).*

## Problem 3: Collector-Feedback BJT Amplifier
*[Appeared in: 2023 Q2(c)]*

**Given:** 
A BJT amplifier has a feedback resistor $R_F = 330\text{ k}\Omega$ connected directly from collector to base.
Components: $R_C = 2.7\text{ k}\Omega$.
h-parameters: $h_{fe} = 120$, $h_{ie} = 1.175\text{ k}\Omega$, $h_{oe} = 20\ \mu\text{S}$. 

**Goal:** Calculate $Z_i, Z_o, A_v,$ and $A_i$.

### Preliminary: Convert $h_{oe}$ to $r_o$

$$
r_o = \frac{1}{h_{oe}} = \frac{1}{20 \times 10^{-6}} = 50\text{ k}\Omega
$$

### Step 1: Effective Load ($R_C'$)
The collector sees its own resistor $R_C$ in parallel with the transistor's internal output resistance $r_o$:

$$
R_C' = R_C \parallel r_o = 2.7\text{ k}\Omega \parallel 50\text{ k}\Omega = \frac{2.7 \times 50}{52.7} \approx \mathbf{2.56\text{ k}\Omega}
$$

### Step 2: Voltage Gain ($A_v$)
This circuit is tricky because $R_F$ provides feedback *and* acts as a parallel load. The formula for a collector-feedback amplifier's gain is:

$$
A_v = -h_{fe} \frac{R_C' \parallel R_F}{h_{ie}}
$$

Since $R_F$ (330k) is massive compared to $R_C'$ (2.56k), their parallel combination is practically just $R_C'$:
$2.56\text{k} \parallel 330\text{k} \approx 2.54\text{ k}\Omega$.

$$
A_v = -120 \left( \frac{2.54\text{ k}}{1.175\text{ k}} \right) \approx -120 \times 2.16 = \boxed{ \mathbf{-259.4} }
$$

### Step 3: Input Impedance ($Z_i$)
Because $R_F$ connects the input to an inverted output, we must use **Miller's Theorem** to find out what it looks like from the input side:

$$
R_{Mi} = \frac{R_F}{1 - A_v} = \frac{330\text{ k}\Omega}{1 - (-259.4)} = \frac{330\text{ k}}{260.4} \approx 1.267\text{ k}\Omega
$$

The total input impedance is the internal $h_{ie}$ in parallel with this Miller resistance:

$$
Z_i = h_{ie} \parallel R_{Mi} = 1.175\text{ k}\Omega \parallel 1.267\text{ k}\Omega = \boxed{ \mathbf{609.6\ \Omega} }
$$

### Step 4: Output Impedance ($Z_o$)
Looking into the output, we see $R_C'$, in parallel with the output Miller resistance $R_{Mo}$. 
Because the gain is huge, $R_{Mo} \approx R_F$. 

$$
Z_o = R_C' \parallel R_F = 2.56\text{ k}\Omega \parallel 330\text{ k}\Omega \approx \boxed{ \mathbf{2.54\text{ k}\Omega} }
$$

### Step 5: Current Gain ($A_i$)
There's a standard formula relating current gain, voltage gain, and impedances:

$$
A_i = A_v \left( \frac{Z_i}{R_C} \right) = -259.4 \times \left( \frac{609.6}{2700} \right) \approx \boxed{ \mathbf{-58.6} }
$$

## ⚡ Exam Tips
- For standard block-diagram problems (like Problem 1), just calculate $D = 1+A\beta$ and multiply/divide. It's free marks.
- For actual circuit problems (like Problem 3), you often have to use **Miller's Theorem** because the feedback resistor physically connects input to output.
