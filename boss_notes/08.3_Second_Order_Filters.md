# Second-Order Filters (Sallen-Key)
> **Topic:** Active Filters | **Priority:** 🟡 IMPORTANT | **Exam Frequency:** 3/8 years

## 1. The Sallen-Key Topology

To build a 2nd-order active filter (which has a steeper $-40\text{ dB/decade}$ roll-off), we use the **Sallen-Key** topology. It uses a single operational amplifier configured as a non-inverting amplifier, along with two resistors and two capacitors in the input network.

For an **equal-component** Sallen-Key Low-Pass Filter, we set $R_1 = R_2 = R$ and $C_1 = C_2 = C$. 

The passband gain of the non-inverting amplifier is:
$$
K = 1 + \frac{R_f}{R_1}
$$

### The Quality Factor ($Q$)
The shape of the filter's frequency response near the cutoff point is entirely determined by its Quality Factor ($Q$).
- **$Q = 0.707$:** Butterworth response (Maximally flat).
- **$Q > 0.707$:** Chebyshev response (Peaking/ripples near cutoff).

For an equal-component Sallen-Key filter, $Q$ is mathematically tied to the amplifier's gain $K$ by this critical formula:
$$
\boxed{ Q = \frac{1}{3 - K} }
$$

### The Stability Constraint
If we increase the gain $K$, the denominator $(3 - K)$ gets smaller, and the Quality Factor $Q$ rises rapidly.
What happens if we set the gain $K = 3$?
$$
Q = \frac{1}{3 - 3} = \frac{1}{0} = \infty
$$
If $Q$ hits infinity (or goes negative if $K > 3$), the filter becomes hopelessly unstable. Instead of filtering signals, it becomes an oscillator and generates its own sine waves! 
Therefore, an equal-component Sallen-Key filter must **strictly** have a gain $K < 3$.

To get a perfectly flat Butterworth response ($Q = 0.707$), we need a very specific gain:
$$
0.707 = \frac{1}{3 - K} \implies K \approx \mathbf{1.586}
$$

## 2. Worked Sallen-Key Design Problem
*[Appeared in: 2020 Q4(c)]*

This is a classic "trick" design question that tests whether you understand the stability constraint.

**Problem Details:** Design a second-order active Sallen-Key low-pass filter with cutoff frequency $f_H = 1.5\text{ kHz}$, passband gain $K_{total} = 5$, and quality factor $Q = 0.707$.

### The Trap
If you try to build a single equal-component Sallen-Key stage with a gain of $5$:
$$
Q = \frac{1}{3 - 5} = -0.5
$$
The negative $Q$ proves the circuit will violently oscillate. It is impossible to achieve both $Q=0.707$ and $K=5$ in a single stage.

### The Engineering Solution
We must split the circuit into two cascaded stages:
1. **Stage 1 (Filter):** A Sallen-Key LPF stage optimized strictly for the frequency response ($Q = 0.707$). This stage will provide a small gain of $K_1 = 1.586$.
2. **Stage 2 (Amplifier):** A standard non-inverting gain stage that makes up the rest of the missing gain ($K_2$) to reach a total of $5$.

### Step 1: Design the Sallen-Key Filter Stage
1. **Select a practical capacitor:** Let $C = 10\text{ nF}$ ($10 \times 10^{-9}\text{ F}$).
2. **Calculate the filter resistors ($R$):**
   $$
   R = \frac{1}{2\pi f_H C} = \frac{1}{2\pi (1500) (10 \times 10^{-9})}
   $$
   $$
   R \approx \mathbf{10.61\text{ k}\Omega}
   $$
3. **Determine the feedback resistors for $K_1 = 1.586$:**
   $$
   K_1 = 1 + \frac{R_f}{R_1} = 1.586 \implies \frac{R_f}{R_1} = 0.586
   $$
   Let the ground resistor $R_1 = 10\text{ k}\Omega$. 
   Then the feedback resistor $R_f = 0.586 \times 10\text{k} = \mathbf{5.86\text{ k}\Omega}$.

### Step 2: Design the Amplifier Stage
1. **Calculate the required gain ($K_2$):**
   The total gain is the product of the cascaded stages: $K_{total} = K_1 \times K_2$.
   $$
   K_2 = \frac{K_{total}}{K_1} = \frac{5}{1.586} \approx \mathbf{3.15}
   $$
2. **Determine the feedback resistors for $K_2 = 3.15$:**
   $$
   K_2 = 1 + \frac{R_{f2}}{R_{12}} = 3.15 \implies \frac{R_{f2}}{R_{12}} = 2.15
   $$
   Let the ground resistor $R_{12} = 10\text{ k}\Omega$.
   Then the feedback resistor $R_{f2} = 2.15 \times 10\text{k} = \mathbf{21.5\text{ k}\Omega}$.

**Final Result:** Connect the output of Stage 1 directly into the input of Stage 2. The combined circuit provides a perfect Butterworth $1.5\text{ kHz}$ cutoff with a safe total gain of $5$.

## ⚡ Exam Tips
- The formula $Q = 1/(3-K)$ is strictly for the *equal-component* topology. If $R_1 \neq R_2$ or $C_1 \neq C_2$, the formula changes drastically. Always assume equal-component for exams unless told otherwise.
- Never blindly apply a gain $> 3$ to a Sallen-Key filter. Showing the cascaded 2-stage solution proves you understand analog circuit design.
