# Differential Amplifier
> **Topic:** Multistage Amplifiers | **Priority:** 🟢 OCCASIONAL | **Exam Frequency:** 2/8 years

## What is a Differential Amplifier?

The differential amplifier is the "input stage" of every operational amplifier. It amplifies the *difference* between two signals while rejecting what's common to both (noise, supply fluctuations, etc.).

As the "All About Electronics" lecture puts it: "The differential amplifier has two inputs. The output is proportional to the *difference* between the two input signals. Any signal that is common to both inputs gets cancelled at the output."

Two key signal types:
- **Differential signal ($V_d$):** The difference between inputs → gets amplified
- **Common-mode signal ($V_{cm}$):** The average of inputs → gets rejected

The ratio of how well it amplifies differences vs. how poorly it passes common signals is the **Common-Mode Rejection Ratio (CMRR)** — a number you want to be as high as possible.

## Symmetrical BJT Differential Pair Circuit

```
             +V_CC                        +V_CC
               |                            |
              [R_C]                        [R_C]
               |                            |
         V_o1 -+                            +- V_o2
               |                            |
          C1  /                              \ C2
   V_i1 --| Q1                           Q2 |-- V_i2
            \                              /
              +--------- E --------+
                         |
                        [R_EE]
                         |
                       -V_EE
```

The two transistors share a common emitter node connected to the negative supply through a tail resistor $R_{EE}$ (or a tail current source in IC designs). The circuit is *symmetrical* — identical $R_C$, identical transistors.

## DC Analysis Under Zero-Input Conditions
*[2017 Q3(c)]*

This is the classic exam problem: both inputs at 0V, find the collector voltages.

**Given:** $V_{CC} = +9\text{ V}$, $V_{EE} = -9\text{ V}$, $R_{EE} = 3.3\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_C = 3.9\text{ k}\Omega$, $V_{BE} = 0.7\text{ V}$.

**Step 1: Emitter voltage.** With both bases grounded (0V):

$$
V_E = V_B - V_{BE} = 0 - 0.7 = -0.7\text{ V}
$$

**Step 2: Total tail current through $R_{EE}$:**

$$
I_{EE} = \frac{V_E - (-V_{EE})}{R_{EE}} = \frac{-0.7 - (-9)}{3.3\text{k}} = \frac{8.3}{3300} \approx 2.515\text{ mA}
$$

**Step 3: Split equally** (perfect symmetry → each transistor gets half):

$$
I_{E1} = I_{E2} = \frac{I_{EE}}{2} = \frac{2.515}{2} = 1.258\text{ mA}
$$

Assuming $I_C \approx I_E$:

$$
I_{C1} = I_{C2} \approx 1.258\text{ mA}
$$

**Step 4: Collector voltages:**

$$
V_{o1} = V_{CC} - I_{C1} R_C = 9 - (1.258 \times 3.9) = 9 - 4.906 = \boxed{4.094\text{ V}}
$$

$$
V_{o2} = V_{CC} - I_{C2} R_C = 9 - 4.906 = \boxed{4.094\text{ V}}
$$

Under zero-input, both outputs are identical — the differential output is zero. The amplifier is "balanced."

## Key Concepts

### Differential Gain ($A_d$) and Common-Mode Gain ($A_{cm}$)

$$
A_d = \frac{-g_m R_C}{2} \quad (\text{single-ended output})
$$

$$
A_{cm} = \frac{-R_C}{2R_{EE}} \quad (\text{single-ended output})
$$

$$
\text{CMRR} = \frac{|A_d|}{|A_{cm}|} = g_m R_{EE}
$$

To maximize CMRR, you want $R_{EE} \to \infty$ — that's why IC diff amps use a **current source** instead of a resistor for the tail. A current source has theoretically infinite impedance, giving infinite CMRR.

### Signal Types

$$
V_d = V_{i1} - V_{i2} \quad \text{(differential input)}
$$

$$
V_{cm} = \frac{V_{i1} + V_{i2}}{2} \quad \text{(common-mode input)}
$$

The output for any input combination:

$$
V_o = A_d \cdot V_d + A_{cm} \cdot V_{cm}
$$

## ⚡ Exam Tips

1. **The tail current splits equally** under zero-input or equal-input conditions. This is the simplest but most important step.
2. **CMRR is linked to $R_{EE}$** — bigger tail resistance = better common-mode rejection. This is why the best designs use a current mirror instead of a resistor.
3. **Don't confuse single-ended vs differential output.** Single-ended = measured from one collector to ground. Differential = measured between the two collectors ($V_{o1} - V_{o2}$).
