# The Cascaded JFET-BJT Amplifier
> **Topic:** Multistage Amplifiers | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** 7/8 years
> **This is the single most important numerical problem in the entire course.**

## Why This Matters

Think about it — if you're cascading two different types of transistors (a JFET and a BJT), you're combining the best of both worlds. The JFET gives you an astronomically high input impedance (in the megaohms), while the BJT gives you a massive voltage gain. The result? A two-stage amplifier that accepts tiny signals without loading the source and amplifies them by hundreds of times.

This exact circuit — with the *exact same component values* — has appeared in **7 out of 8 final exams** (2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2022, 2023, 2024). It is almost always **Question 1**. If you memorize this solution, you're guaranteed 5-8 marks before you even flip the page.

```
      |             |            |
      +---+         +-----+      +---+------||------+ V_o
      |   |               |      |   |     C_out
    D |   |  C_C (coupling)|   C |   |
     ---  +----||----+----+---|\  |   |
  G |   |             |       | \|   |
 ---+   |            [ ] R_B2 |  ----+

```

### Component Values (Memorize These!)

**Stage 1 — JFET Common-Source:**
- $R_G = 3.3\text{ M}\Omega$, $R_D = 2.4\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_S = 680\;\Omega$ (bypassed by $C_S$)
- JFET Parameters: $I_{DSS} = 10\text{ mA}$, $V_P = -4\text{ V}$

**Stage 2 — BJT Common-Emitter:**
- $R_{B1} = 15\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_{B2} = 4.7\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_C = 2.2\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_E = 1\text{ k}\Omega$ (bypassed by $C_E$)
- BJT Parameters: $\beta = 200$, $V_{BE} = 0.7\text{ V}$

**Supply:** $V_{DD} = V_{CC} = +20\text{ V}$

## The Complete Solution — Step by Step

### Step 1: DC Bias of the JFET Stage

The JFET gate draws zero DC current ($I_G \approx 0$). This is a self-bias configuration — the voltage at the gate is 0V (because $I_G \times R_G = 0$), and the source sits at a positive voltage due to $I_D$ flowing through $R_S$. This creates a negative $V_{GS}$, which is exactly what we need to bias an n-channel JFET.

The gate-source voltage is:

$$
V_{GS} = 0 - I_D R_S = -680 \, I_D
$$

Now substitute into **Shockley's equation** (this is the fundamental JFET equation — it describes how drain current depends on gate-source voltage):

$$
I_D = I_{DSS} \left(1 - \frac{V_{GS}}{V_P}\right)^2 = 10\text{ mA} \left(1 - \frac{-680\,I_D}{-4}\right)^2
$$

$$
I_D = 0.01 \left(1 - 170\,I_D\right)^2
$$

Expand the square:

$$
I_D = 0.01 \left(1 - 340\,I_D + 28900\,I_D^2\right)
$$

$$
I_D = 0.01 - 3.4\,I_D + 289\,I_D^2
$$

Rearrange into standard quadratic form:

$$
289\,I_D^2 - 4.4\,I_D + 0.01 = 0
$$

Apply the quadratic formula:

$$
I_D = \frac{4.4 \pm \sqrt{4.4^2 - 4(289)(0.01)}}{2(289)} = \frac{4.4 \pm \sqrt{19.36 - 11.56}}{578} = \frac{4.4 \pm 2.793}{578}
$$

Two solutions:
- $I_D \approx 12.4\text{ mA}$ → **Reject** (exceeds $I_{DSS} = 10\text{ mA}$, physically impossible)
- $I_D \approx 2.78\text{ mA}$ → **Accept** ✅

Now calculate the quiescent operating point:

$$
V_{GSQ} = -680 \times 2.78\text{ mA} \approx -1.89\text{ V}
$$

And the all-important **transconductance** $g_m$ (this tells you how much the drain current changes per volt change at the gate — it's the JFET's "gain factor"):

$$
g_m = \frac{2\,I_{DSS}}{|V_P|}\left(1 - \frac{V_{GSQ}}{V_P}\right) = \frac{2 \times 10\text{ mA}}{4}\left(1 - \frac{-1.89}{-4}\right) = 5\text{ mS} \times 0.5275 \approx 2.64\text{ mS}
$$

---

### Step 2: DC Bias of the BJT Stage

Use Thevenin's theorem to simplify the base biasing network. We replace $R_{B1}$ and $R_{B2}$ with their Thevenin equivalent:

$$
V_{th} = V_{CC} \times \frac{R_{B2}}{R_{B1} + R_{B2}} = 20\text{ V} \times \frac{4.7\text{k}}{15\text{k} + 4.7\text{k}} = 20 \times \frac{4.7}{19.7} \approx 4.77\text{ V}
$$

$$
R_{th} = R_{B1} \| R_{B2} = \frac{15\text{k} \times 4.7\text{k}}{15\text{k} + 4.7\text{k}} = 3.58\text{ k}\Omega
$$

Apply KVL around the base-emitter loop (walking from $V_{th}$ through $R_{th}$, the base-emitter junction, and $R_E$ to ground):

$$
V_{th} - I_B R_{th} - V_{BE} - I_E R_E = 0
$$

Since $I_E \approx (\beta+1) I_B \approx \beta I_B$ for large $\beta$:

$$
4.77 - I_E \left(\frac{3.58\text{k}}{200}\right) - 0.7 - I_E (1\text{k}) = 0
$$

$$
4.07 = I_E \times 1.018\text{k} \quad \Rightarrow \quad I_{EQ} \approx 4.0\text{ mA}
$$

The dynamic emitter resistance (this is the BJT's small-signal "internal resistance" at the emitter — it determines the gain):

$$
r_e = \frac{26\text{ mV}}{I_{EQ}} = \frac{26}{4.0} = 6.5\;\Omega
$$

---

### Step 3: AC Analysis — The Gains

Now we switch to small-signal AC analysis. All DC supplies become ground, all coupling/bypass capacitors become short circuits.

**Input impedance of Stage 2 ($Z_{i2}$):**

$$
Z_{i2} = R_{th} \| \beta r_e = 3.58\text{k} \| (200 \times 6.5) = 3.58\text{k} \| 1.3\text{k} \approx 953\;\Omega
$$

Why does this matter? Because Stage 2's input impedance is the *load* that Stage 1 sees at its drain. It pulls down Stage 1's gain.

**Stage 1 Voltage Gain ($A_{v1}$):**

The JFET CS stage gain is $-g_m$ times the effective load at the drain. The drain sees $R_D$ in parallel with $Z_{i2}$ (the next stage's input):

$$
A_{v1} = -g_m (R_D \| Z_{i2}) = -2.64\text{ mS} \times (2.4\text{k} \| 953) = -2.64\text{ mS} \times 682.3 \approx -1.8
$$

The negative sign means phase inversion — when the input goes up, the output goes down.

**Stage 2 Voltage Gain ($A_{v2}$):**

The BJT CE stage gain with bypassed emitter is simply:

$$
A_{v2} = -\frac{R_C}{r_e} = -\frac{2.2\text{k}}{6.5} \approx -338.5
$$

Again, another 180° phase inversion.

**Overall Voltage Gain ($A_v$):**

$$
A_v = A_{v1} \times A_{v2} = (-1.8) \times (-338.5) \approx \boxed{609.3}
$$

The two inversions cancel out! The overall output is **in-phase** with the input ($0°$ or $360°$ total phase shift).

---

### Step 4: Input and Output Impedances

**Overall Input Impedance ($Z_i$):**

Looking into the JFET gate, the input impedance is just the gate resistor (since the JFET gate draws no current):

$$
Z_i = R_G = 3.3\text{ M}\Omega
$$

This is enormous — the amplifier barely loads the signal source at all.

**Overall Output Impedance ($Z_o$):**

Looking back into the BJT collector:

$$
Z_o = R_C = 2.2\text{ k}\Omega
$$

---

### Step 5: Output Waveform

For $v_i(t) = 10\sin(\omega t)\text{ mV}$:

$$
v_o(t) = A_v \times v_i(t) = 609.3 \times 10\sin(\omega t)\text{ mV} \approx 6.09\sin(\omega t)\text{ V}
$$

- The output is **in-phase** with the input (two $180°$ inversions = $360° = 0°$)
- Amplitude is amplified from 10 mV to about 6.09 V

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

1. **Don't forget to reject the larger $I_D$ root.** If you get $I_D > I_{DSS}$, it's physically impossible — always pick the smaller root.
2. **Loading effect is KEY.** The gain of Stage 1 is NOT just $-g_m R_D$. You must account for Stage 2's input impedance: $A_{v1} = -g_m(R_D \| Z_{i2})$. Forgetting this is the #1 mark-losing mistake.
3. **Phase matters for waveform sketching.** Two CE/CS inversions = in-phase output. If they ask you to sketch the output wave, make sure it's *in-phase* with the input, not inverted.
4. **$Z_i$ is just $R_G$.** Don't overcomplicate this. The JFET gate draws zero current, so the input impedance is simply the gate resistor.
