# Wien Bridge Oscillator
> **Topic:** Oscillators | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** 5/8 years

## How It Satisfies Barkhausen

Unlike the Phase-Shift oscillator, the Wien Bridge oscillator uses a **Non-Inverting Amplifier**. 
Because the amplifier is non-inverting, it contributes exactly $0^\circ$ of phase shift. To satisfy the Barkhausen criterion, the feedback network must also contribute exactly $0^\circ$ of phase shift.

To achieve this, the feedback network uses a "Wien Selective Bridge" connected to the non-inverting input.

---

## 1. Wien Selective Bridge Analysis
*[Appeared in: 2020 Q5(a), 2020 Q5(c)]*

### The Feedback Circuit
The feedback path contains two RC networks:
1. A **Series** RC branch ($Z_s$) connecting the output to the non-inverting input.
2. A **Parallel** RC branch ($Z_p$) connecting the non-inverting input to ground.

This forms an AC voltage divider. The transfer function $\beta = V_f / V_o$ is:
$$
\beta(s) = \frac{Z_p}{Z_s + Z_p}
$$

By substituting $s = j\omega$ and assuming both resistors are $R$ and both capacitors are $C$, the complex algebra simplifies to:
$$
\beta = \frac{1}{3 + j \left( \omega RC - \frac{1}{\omega RC} \right)}
$$

### Deriving the Resonant Frequency ($f_o$)
According to Barkhausen, the phase shift must be exactly $0^\circ$. This means the imaginary component in the denominator must be completely eliminated.
$$
\omega RC - \frac{1}{\omega RC} = 0
$$

Move the negative term over:
$$
\omega RC = \frac{1}{\omega RC}
$$

Multiply by $\omega$:
$$
\omega^2 RC = \frac{1}{RC} \implies \omega^2 = \frac{1}{R^2 C^2}
$$

Solve for $\omega$:
$$
\omega = \frac{1}{RC}
$$

Since $\omega = 2\pi f_o$, we get the standard cutoff frequency formula:
$$
\boxed{ f_o = \frac{1}{2\pi RC} }
$$

### Deriving the Gain Requirement ($|A| \ge 3$)
Now that the imaginary part is zero, what is the feedback attenuation at this exact frequency? Let's plug it back into the $\beta$ formula:
$$
\beta = \frac{1}{3 + j(0)} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{3}}
$$

The Barkhausen amplitude condition states $|A\beta| \ge 1$.
$$
A \times \left(\frac{1}{3}\right) \ge 1 \implies \boxed{ A \ge 3 }
$$

Because the op-amp is in a non-inverting configuration, its gain is defined by $A = 1 + \frac{R_F}{R_1}$.
$$
1 + \frac{R_F}{R_1} \ge 3 \implies \frac{R_F}{R_1} \ge 2 \implies \mathbf{R_F \ge 2 R_1}
$$

**Conclusion:** To sustain oscillations, the feedback resistor must be at least twice as large as the ground resistor.

## 2. Worked Design Problem
*[Appeared in: 2018 Q8(c), 2020 Q5(c)]*

**Problem Details:**
Design an op-amp based Wien bridge oscillator to operate at a frequency of $f_o = 10\text{ kHz}$.

### Step 1: Select a Practical Capacitor
Always pick a standard capacitor value to start.
Let **$C = 10\text{ nF}$** ($0.01\ \mu\text{F} = 10 \times 10^{-9}\text{ F}$).

### Step 2: Calculate the Required Feedback Resistor ($R$)
Using the derived frequency formula:
$$
f_o = \frac{1}{2\pi RC}
$$
Rearrange to solve for $R$:
$$
R = \frac{1}{2\pi f_o C}
$$
$$
R = \frac{1}{2\pi (10,000) (10 \times 10^{-9})} = \frac{1}{2\pi \times 10^{-4}} = \frac{10000}{2\pi} \approx \mathbf{1591.5\ \Omega}
$$
We will select a standard resistor value of **$1.6\text{ k}\Omega$** for the two Wien bridge resistors.

### Step 3: Design the Non-Inverting Amplifier Gain
We proved the gain must be at least 3, meaning $R_F \ge 2 R_1$.

Let's pick a standard value for the ground resistor $R_1$:
Let **$R_1 = 10\text{ k}\Omega$**.

Therefore, the minimum required feedback resistor is:
$$
R_F = 2 \times 10\text{ k}\Omega = \mathbf{20\text{ k}\Omega}
$$

To guarantee oscillations actually start, it is common practice to select a slightly larger standard value for $R_F$. We will specify **$R_F = 22\text{ k}\Omega$**. (An incandescent bulb or thermistor is typically used in real circuits to automatically lower this back to exactly 20k as the amplitude grows).

## ⚡ Exam Tips
- The mathematical derivation is much simpler than the Phase-Shift oscillator. Just remember $\omega RC - 1/\omega RC = 0$. 
- The fact that the Wien bridge uses a **Non-Inverting** amplifier (gain $\ge 3$) while the Phase-Shift uses an **Inverting** amplifier (gain $\ge 29$) is a very common multiple-choice or short-answer trap.
- In design questions, clearly state your arbitrary capacitor choice (e.g., "Let $C = 10\text{nF}$"). If you forget to state this, your math will look like magic numbers.
