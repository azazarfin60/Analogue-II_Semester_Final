# Wien Bridge Oscillator
> **Topic:** Oscillators | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** 5/8 years

## Basic Knowledge: Zero Phase Shift

We know the Barkhausen criterion demands a total loop phase shift of exactly 360 degrees. 360 degrees is mathematically identical to 0 degrees.

Unlike the Phase-Shift oscillator, the Wien Bridge oscillator uses a **Non-Inverting Amplifier**. 

Because the amplifier is non-inverting, it does not flip the signal. It contributes exactly 0 degrees of phase shift. Therefore, to satisfy the Barkhausen criterion, the feedback network must also contribute exactly 0 degrees of phase shift at the desired frequency.

To achieve this perfect 0 degree shift, the feedback network uses a special circuit called a "Wien Selective Bridge." This bridge connects the output directly back to the non-inverting input.

---

## 1. Wien Selective Bridge Analysis
*[Appeared in: 2020 Q5(a), 2020 Q5(c)]*

### The Feedback Circuit
The feedback path contains two Resistor-Capacitor (RC) networks:
1. A **Series** RC branch ($Z_s$) connecting the output to the non-inverting input.
2. A **Parallel** RC branch ($Z_p$) connecting the non-inverting input to ground.

This forms a complex AC voltage divider. The transfer function $\beta = V_f / V_o$ is simply:

$$
\beta(s) = \frac{Z_p}{Z_s + Z_p}
$$

By substituting $s = j\omega$ and assuming both resistors are $R$ and both capacitors are $C$, the complex algebra simplifies beautifully to:

$$
\beta = \frac{1}{3 + j \left( \omega RC - \frac{1}{\omega RC} \right)}
$$

### Deriving the Resonant Frequency ($f_o$)
According to Barkhausen, the phase shift must be exactly 0 degrees. This means the imaginary component in the denominator must be completely eliminated.

$$
\omega RC - \frac{1}{\omega RC} = 0
$$

Move the negative term over:

$$
\omega RC = \frac{1}{\omega RC}
$$

Multiply both sides by $\omega$:

$$
\omega^2 RC = \frac{1}{RC} \implies \omega^2 = \frac{1}{R^2 C^2}
$$

Solve for $\omega$:

$$
\omega = \frac{1}{RC}
$$

Since $\omega = 2\pi f_o$, we get the standard cutoff frequency formula:

$$
\boxed{ f_o = \frac{1}{2\pi RC} }
$$

### Deriving the Gain Requirement ($|A| \ge 3$)
Now that the imaginary part is zero, what is the feedback attenuation at this exact frequency? Let's plug it back into the $\beta$ formula:

$$
\beta = \frac{1}{3 + j(0)} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{3}}
$$

The Barkhausen amplitude condition states $|A\beta| \ge 1$.

$$
A \times \left(\frac{1}{3}\right) \ge 1 \implies \boxed{ A \ge 3 }
$$

Because the op-amp is in a standard non-inverting configuration, its gain is defined by $A = 1 + \frac{R_F}{R_1}$.

$$
1 + \frac{R_F}{R_1} \ge 3 \implies \frac{R_F}{R_1} \ge 2 \implies \mathbf{R_F \ge 2 R_1}
$$

**Conclusion:** To sustain continuous oscillations, the feedback resistor must be at least twice as large as the ground resistor.

## 2. Worked Design Problem
*[Appeared in: 2018 Q8(c), 2020 Q5(c)]*

**Problem Details:**
Design an op-amp based Wien bridge oscillator to operate at a frequency of $f_o = 10\text{ kHz}$.

### Step 1: Select a Practical Capacitor
Always pick a standard capacitor value to start your design.
Let **$C = 10\text{ nF}$** ($0.01\ \mu\text{F} = 10 \times 10^{-9}\text{ F}$).

### Step 2: Calculate the Required Feedback Resistor ($R$)
Use the derived frequency formula:

$$
f_o = \frac{1}{2\pi RC}
$$

Rearrange to solve for $R$:

$$
R = \frac{1}{2\pi f_o C}
$$

$$
R = \frac{1}{2\pi (10,000) (10 \times 10^{-9})} = \frac{1}{2\pi \times 10^{-4}} = \frac{10000}{2\pi} \approx \mathbf{1591.5\ \Omega}
$$

We will select a standard resistor value of **$1.6\text{ k}\Omega$** for the two Wien bridge resistors.

### Step 3: Design the Non-Inverting Amplifier Gain
We proved the gain must be at least 3. This means $R_F \ge 2 R_1$.

Let's pick a standard value for the ground resistor $R_1$:
Let **$R_1 = 10\text{ k}\Omega$**.

Therefore, the minimum required feedback resistor is:

$$
R_F = 2 \times 10\text{ k}\Omega = \mathbf{20\text{ k}\Omega}
$$

To guarantee the oscillations actually start when power is turned on, it is common practice to select a slightly larger standard value for $R_F$. We will specify **$R_F = 22\text{ k}\Omega$**. 

*(Note: Real-world circuits often use an incandescent bulb or thermistor to automatically lower this gain back to exactly 3 as the wave amplitude grows).*

## ⚡ Exam Tips
- **Simple math.** The mathematical derivation is much simpler than the Phase-Shift oscillator. Just remember to set $\omega RC - 1/\omega RC = 0$. 
- **The big trap.** The fact that the Wien bridge uses a **Non-Inverting** amplifier (gain $\ge 3$) while the Phase-Shift uses an **Inverting** amplifier (gain $\ge 29$) is an extremely common multiple-choice trap. Do not mix them up.
- **State your choices.** In design questions, clearly state your arbitrary capacitor choice (for example, "Let $C = 10\text{nF}$"). If you forget to state this, your math will look like magic numbers and you will lose marks.
