# Feedback Worked Numericals
> **Topic:** Feedback Amplifiers | **Priority:** 🟡 IMPORTANT | **Exam Frequency:** 3/8 years

## Basic Knowledge: Solving Feedback Problems

When solving feedback numericals in exams, you will usually face two types of problems:

1. **Block Diagram Problems:** The exam gives you the raw, open-loop numbers ($A$, $R_i$, $R_o$) and the feedback fraction ($\beta$). Your job is to find the final, closed-loop values. These are easy. You just calculate the Desensitivity Factor ($D = 1 + A\beta$) and multiply or divide.
2. **Circuit Level Problems:** The exam gives you a schematic of a real transistor circuit with a feedback resistor. These are harder. You must use Miller's Theorem to split the feedback resistor apart. Then you analyze it like a standard amplifier.

Always follow a clear, step-by-step approach. Write down the given values first. Always calculate the open-loop gain ($A$) before applying any feedback math.

## Problem 1: Voltage-Series Parameter Extraction
*[Appeared in: 2018 Q6(b)]*

**Given Values:** 
A voltage-series feedback amplifier has these open-loop parameters: 
- Voltage gain $A = -100$
- Input impedance $R_i = 10\text{ k}\Omega$
- Output impedance $R_o = 20\text{ k}\Omega$
- The feedback factor is $\beta = -0.1$

**Goal:** Calculate the closed-loop parameters ($A_f, R_{if}, R_{of}$).

### Step 1: Calculate the Desensitivity Factor ($D$)
Always calculate this first. It is the core multiplier and divider for everything else.

$$
D = 1 + A\beta = 1 + (-100)(-0.1) = 1 + 10 = \mathbf{11}
$$

### Step 2: Closed-Loop Voltage Gain ($A_f$)
Negative feedback always reduces the voltage gain. You divide by $D$.

$$
A_f = \frac{A}{D} = \frac{-100}{11} \approx \boxed{ \mathbf{-9.09} }
$$

### Step 3: Closed-Loop Input Impedance ($R_{if}$)
This is a "Series" input connection. Series means the impedance increases. You multiply by $D$.

$$
R_{if} = R_i \times D = 10\text{ k}\Omega \times 11 = \boxed{ \mathbf{110\text{ k}\Omega} }
$$

### Step 4: Closed-Loop Output Impedance ($R_{of}$)
This is a "Voltage" output connection. That means it samples in parallel (shunt). Parallel means the impedance decreases. You divide by $D$.

$$
R_{of} = \frac{R_o}{D} = \frac{20\text{ k}\Omega}{11} \approx \boxed{ \mathbf{1.818\text{ k}\Omega} }
$$

## Problem 2: JFET CS Feedback Loop Analysis
*[Appeared in: 2017 Q2(b)]*

**Given Values:** 
A JFET Common-Source (CS) amplifier has $g_m = 5800\ \mu\text{S}$. 
It uses a feedback resistor divider: $R_1 = 100\text{ k}\Omega$ and $R_2 = 20\text{ k}\Omega$. $R_2$ connects across the input to ground. $R_1$ feeds from the output back to $R_2$. 
Load components: $R_D = 10\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_L = 10\text{ k}\Omega$. 

**Goal:** Calculate the absolute change in gain magnitude caused by adding the feedback loop.

### Step 1: Gain Without Feedback ($A$)
Imagine the feedback loop is disconnected. The amplifier only sees the drain resistor and the load resistor.
We find the AC load resistance at the drain:

$$
R_L' = R_D \parallel R_L = 10\text{ k}\Omega \parallel 10\text{ k}\Omega = 5\text{ k}\Omega
$$

The open-loop gain of a standard CS amplifier is:

$$
A = -g_m R_L' = -(5800 \times 10^{-6}\text{ S}) \times 5000\ \Omega = \mathbf{-29}
$$

### Step 2: The Feedback Factor ($\beta$)
The feedback network is a simple voltage divider made of $R_1$ and $R_2$. The voltage returned to the source is the voltage across $R_2$:

$$
\beta = -\frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2} = -\frac{20\text{ k}}{100\text{ k} + 20\text{ k}} = -\frac{20}{120} \approx \mathbf{-0.1667}
$$

> **Note:** The negative sign indicates it is an inverting feedback loop relative to the input.

### Step 3: Gain With Feedback ($A_f$)
Now we apply the standard feedback formula:

$$
A_f = \frac{A}{1 + A\beta} = \frac{-29}{1 + (-29)(-0.1667)} = \frac{-29}{1 + 4.834} = \frac{-29}{5.834} \approx \mathbf{-4.97}
$$

### Step 4: The Change
The question asks for the absolute change in gain magnitude.

- Gain magnitude without feedback: $|-29| = 29$
- Gain magnitude with feedback: $|-4.97| = 4.97$
- Absolute change: $29 - 4.97 = \boxed{ \mathbf{24.03} }$ 

> **Note:** The feedback reduced the gain by an enormous 82.8 percent!

## Problem 3: Collector-Feedback BJT Amplifier
*[Appeared in: 2023 Q2(c)]*

**Given Values:** 
A BJT amplifier has a feedback resistor $R_F = 330\text{ k}\Omega$ connected directly from the collector back to the base.
Components: $R_C = 2.7\text{ k}\Omega$.
h-parameters: $h_{fe} = 120$, $h_{ie} = 1.175\text{ k}\Omega$, $h_{oe} = 20\ \mu\text{S}$. 

**Goal:** Calculate $Z_i, Z_o, A_v,$ and $A_i$.

### Preliminary: Convert $h_{oe}$ to $r_o$
We must convert the output conductance into a resistance.

$$
r_o = \frac{1}{h_{oe}} = \frac{1}{20 \times 10^{-6}} = 50\text{ k}\Omega
$$

### Step 1: Effective Load ($R_C'$)
The collector sees its own resistor $R_C$ in parallel with the transistor's internal output resistance $r_o$:

$$
R_C' = R_C \parallel r_o = 2.7\text{ k}\Omega \parallel 50\text{ k}\Omega = \frac{2.7 \times 50}{52.7} \approx \mathbf{2.56\text{ k}\Omega}
$$

### Step 2: Voltage Gain ($A_v$)
This circuit is tricky. $R_F$ provides feedback but it also acts as a parallel load. The formula for a collector-feedback amplifier's gain is:

$$
A_v = -h_{fe} \frac{R_C' \parallel R_F}{h_{ie}}
$$

The resistor $R_F$ (330k) is massive compared to $R_C'$ (2.56k). Their parallel combination is practically just $R_C'$.
$2.56\text{k} \parallel 330\text{k} \approx 2.54\text{ k}\Omega$.

$$
A_v = -120 \left( \frac{2.54\text{ k}}{1.175\text{ k}} \right) \approx -120 \times 2.16 = \boxed{ \mathbf{-259.4} }
$$

### Step 3: Input Impedance ($Z_i$)
The resistor $R_F$ connects the input to an inverted output. We must use **Miller's Theorem** to find out what it looks like from the input side:

$$
R_{Mi} = \frac{R_F}{1 - A_v} = \frac{330\text{ k}\Omega}{1 - (-259.4)} = \frac{330\text{ k}}{260.4} \approx 1.267\text{ k}\Omega
$$

The total input impedance is the internal $h_{ie}$ in parallel with this Miller resistance:

$$
Z_i = h_{ie} \parallel R_{Mi} = 1.175\text{ k}\Omega \parallel 1.267\text{ k}\Omega = \boxed{ \mathbf{609.6\ \Omega} }
$$

### Step 4: Output Impedance ($Z_o$)
Looking back into the output, we see $R_C'$ in parallel with the output Miller resistance $R_{Mo}$. 
Because the gain is huge, the Miller rule says $R_{Mo} \approx R_F$. 

$$
Z_o = R_C' \parallel R_F = 2.56\text{ k}\Omega \parallel 330\text{ k}\Omega \approx \boxed{ \mathbf{2.54\text{ k}\Omega} }
$$

### Step 5: Current Gain ($A_i$)
We use the standard formula relating current gain, voltage gain, and impedances:

$$
A_i = A_v \left( \frac{Z_i}{R_C} \right) = -259.4 \times \left( \frac{609.6}{2700} \right) \approx \boxed{ \mathbf{-58.6} }
$$

## ⚡ Exam Tips
- **Block diagram problems.** For questions like Problem 1, just calculate $D = 1+A\beta$ and then multiply or divide. It is easy marks.
- **Circuit problems.** For actual circuit problems like Problem 3, you often must use Miller's Theorem. This is because the feedback resistor physically bridges the input and output.
