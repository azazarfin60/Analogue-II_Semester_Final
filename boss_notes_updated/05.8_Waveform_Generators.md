# Non-Linear Waveform Generators
> **Topic:** Op-Amp Applications | **Priority:** 🟢 OCCASIONAL | **Exam Frequency:** 1/8 years

## Basic Knowledge: Amplifiers vs. Oscillators

Most op-amp circuits we study (like amplifiers, integrators, and summers) take an input signal and change it. To do this predictably, they use **negative feedback**. Negative feedback keeps the op-amp stable and in its linear region. In the linear region, the output is directly proportional to the input.

Waveform generators are different. They do not have an input signal. They generate their own signal from nothing. To do this, they use **positive feedback**. 

Positive feedback deliberately drives the op-amp into complete instability. It forces the output to instantly snap back and forth between the maximum positive voltage ($+V_{sat}$) and the maximum negative voltage ($-V_{sat}$). Because the output is always saturating at the limits, the op-amp is operating entirely in its non-linear region.

## 1. Pulse Generator (Astable Multivibrator)
*[Appeared in: 2017 Q6(c)]*

A standard astable multivibrator generates a perfectly symmetrical square wave. The wave is ON 50 percent of the time and OFF 50 percent of the time. 

A **Pulse Generator** is simply a modified astable multivibrator. It generates an *asymmetrical* square wave (for example, 10 percent ON, 90 percent OFF). This creates a train of very narrow pulses.

### How it works:
1. **The Core Circuit:** The core is a Schmitt Trigger. It uses positive feedback to create upper and lower thresholds. It also has a resistor-capacitor (RC) timing circuit connected to the inverting terminal.
2. **Symmetrical Operation:** The capacitor $C$ charges up through a feedback resistor $R$ until it hits the Upper Trigger Point ($V_{UT}$). The op-amp output then snaps to $-V_{sat}$. The capacitor then discharges through that *same* resistor $R$ until it hits the Lower Trigger Point ($V_{LT}$). The output snaps back to $+V_{sat}$. Since the charge time perfectly equals the discharge time, you get a 50 percent square wave.
3. **The Pulse Modification:** To make a pulse generator, we place a diode $D$ in parallel with a portion of the feedback resistor network.
   - **Charging Phase:** When the output is $+V_{sat}$, the diode is forward-biased. It creates a short-circuit bypass around the main resistor. The capacitor charges *extremely rapidly* through this low-resistance path.
   - **Discharging Phase:** When the output is $-V_{sat}$, the diode is reverse-biased. It acts as an open circuit. The capacitor is now forced to discharge *slowly* through the full high-resistance path.
4. **The Result:** The op-amp spends very little time at $+V_{sat}$ because of the fast charge. It spends a long time at $-V_{sat}$ because of the slow discharge. This produces a train of narrow positive pulses.

## 2. Triangular Wave Generator
*[Appeared in: 2017 Q6(c)]*

A triangular wave generator is created by cascading two different op-amp circuits together in a continuous loop:
1. **A Schmitt Trigger** (Astable Multivibrator)
2. **A Practical Integrator**

### How it works:
1. The **Schmitt Trigger** is designed to output a harsh, digital square wave ($\pm V_{sat}$).
2. This square wave feeds directly into the input of the **Integrator**.
3. We know from calculus that the integral of a constant is a linear ramp ($y = mx$). 
   - When the square wave is at $+V_{sat}$, the integrator integrates a positive constant. This produces a steady, linear **downward ramp** (because the integrator is an *inverting* integrator).
   - When the square wave flips to $-V_{sat}$, the integrator integrates a negative constant. This produces a steady, linear **upward ramp**.
4. The output of the integrator feeds *back* into the input of the Schmitt trigger. When the ramp gets high enough, it triggers the Schmitt trigger to flip states.
5. **The Result:** The constant integration of alternating positive and negative constants produces a highly symmetrical, perfectly straight triangular waveform.

*(Note: The frequency and amplitude of the triangular wave are controlled by the RC time constant of the integrator and the threshold voltages of the Schmitt trigger).*

## ⚡ Exam Tips
- **Keyword for Pulse.** The key concept for the Pulse Generator is "asymmetric charge and discharge paths using a diode."
- **Keyword for Triangle.** The key concept for the Triangular Wave Generator is "cascading a square wave Schmitt trigger into an inverting integrator."
