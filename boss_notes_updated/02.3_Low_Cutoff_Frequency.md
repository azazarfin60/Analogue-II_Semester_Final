# Low-Cutoff Frequency Calculations
> **Topic:** Frequency Response | **Priority:** 🟡 IMPORTANT | **Exam Frequency:** 5/8 years

## Basic Knowledge: Capacitors as Filters

Most amplifiers use capacitors to work properly. They use them for three main reasons:
1. **Input Coupling ($C_{in}$ or $C_G$):** This stops the DC bias inside the amplifier from leaking back into the signal source.
2. **Output Coupling ($C_{out}$ or $C_C$):** This stops the DC bias from going into the load (like a speaker).
3. **Bypass ($C_E$ or $C_S$):** This provides a fast path for AC signals to ground. It skips the emitter or source resistor. This maximizes the voltage gain.

At mid-band frequencies, these capacitors act like perfect short circuits. The AC signal passes straight through them. 

But capacitors resist changes in voltage. Their resistance to AC signals is called reactance ($X_c = 1 / 2\pi f C$). As the frequency ($f$) drops, the reactance goes up. 

At very low frequencies, the reactance becomes huge. The capacitors act like roadblocks. They block the AC signal. The voltage gain drops. 

Each capacitor in the circuit creates a high-pass filter with its surrounding resistors. We must find the cutoff frequency for each capacitor. The capacitor that cuts off the highest frequency becomes the bottleneck. This highest value sets the overall lower cutoff frequency ($f_L$) for the whole amplifier.

To find the cutoff frequency for any single capacitor, we use this general formula:

$$
f = \frac{1}{2\pi R_{eq} C}
$$

Here, $R_{eq}$ is the Thevenin equivalent resistance connected to that specific capacitor.

## Worked Numerical 1: JFET Common-Source
*[Appeared in: 2024 Q4(b), 2022 Q3(c), 2021 Q3(b)]*

**Given Values:** 
- JFET parameters: $g_m = 2\text{ mS}$
- Resistors: $R_{sig} = 10\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_G = 1\text{ M}\Omega$, $R_D = 4.7\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_S = 1\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_L = 2.2\text{ k}\Omega$
- Capacitors: Input $C_G = 0.01\ \mu\text{F}$, Output $C_C = 0.5\ \mu\text{F}$, Bypass $C_S = 2\ \mu\text{F}$

**Goal:** Find the overall lower cutoff frequency $f_L$.

### Step 1: Input Gate Coupling Capacitor ($C_G$)
We must find the total resistance connected in series with $C_G$. It sits between the signal source resistance ($R_{sig}$) and the gate resistance ($R_G$). The JFET gate draws zero current. The input impedance looking straight into the gate is practically infinite. 

So, we just add the two series resistors:

$$
R_{in(G)} = R_{sig} + R_G = 10\text{ k}\Omega + 1\text{ M}\Omega = 1.01\text{ M}\Omega
$$

Now calculate the cutoff frequency:

$$
f_{LG} = \frac{1}{2\pi R_{in(G)} C_G} = \frac{1}{2\pi \times (1.01 \times 10^6) \times (0.01 \times 10^{-6})} \approx \mathbf{15.76\text{ Hz}}
$$

### Step 2: Output Coupling Capacitor ($C_C$)
We must find the total resistance connected in series with $C_C$. It sits between the drain of the JFET and the load $R_L$. Looking back into the drain, it sees $R_D$. Looking forward, it sees $R_L$.

We add these two resistors together:

$$
R_{out(C)} = R_D + R_L = 4.7\text{ k}\Omega + 2.2\text{ k}\Omega = 6.9\text{ k}\Omega
$$

Now calculate the cutoff frequency:

$$
f_{LC} = \frac{1}{2\pi R_{out(C)} C_C} = \frac{1}{2\pi \times (6.9 \times 10^3) \times (0.5 \times 10^{-6})} \approx \mathbf{46.13\text{ Hz}}
$$

### Step 3: Source Bypass Capacitor ($C_S$)
This calculation is slightly tricky. $C_S$ sits in parallel with $R_S$. We must also look up into the JFET source terminal. The internal resistance looking up into the source is exactly $1/g_m$. 

The equivalent resistance is $R_S$ in parallel with $1/g_m$:

$$
R_{eq(S)} = R_S \parallel \left(\frac{1}{g_m}\right) = 1\text{ k}\Omega \parallel \left(\frac{1}{2\text{ mS}}\right) = 1000\ \Omega \parallel 500\ \Omega = 333.33\ \Omega
$$

Now calculate the cutoff frequency:

$$
f_{LS} = \frac{1}{2\pi R_{eq(S)} C_S} = \frac{1}{2\pi \times (333.33) \times (2 \times 10^{-6})} \approx \mathbf{238.73\text{ Hz}}
$$

### Step 4: Overall Lower Cutoff Frequency
The overall $f_L$ is the highest individual cutoff frequency. It forms the tightest bottleneck for low-frequency signals.

$$
f_L \approx \max(f_{LG}, f_{LC}, f_{LS}) = \max(15.76, 46.13, 238.73) = \boxed{ \mathbf{238.73\text{ Hz}} }
$$

> **Note:** In most circuits, the bypass capacitor dictates the lower cutoff frequency. It usually sees the lowest equivalent resistance.

## Worked Numerical 2: BJT Common-Emitter
*[Appeared in: 2019 Q3(b), 2020 Q3(c), CT2]*

**Given Values:**
- BJT parameters: $\beta = 100$, $V_{BE} = 0.7\text{ V}$
- Resistors: $R_1 = 40\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_2 = 10\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_C = 4\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_E = 2\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_{sig} = 0\ \Omega$, $R_L = 2.2\text{ k}\Omega$
- Supply: $V_{CC} = 20\text{ V}$
- Capacitors: Input $C_{in} = 10\ \mu\text{F}$, Output $C_{out} = 1\ \mu\text{F}$, Bypass $C_E = 20\ \mu\text{F}$

**Goal:** Find the overall lower cutoff frequency $f_L$.

### Preliminary Step: DC Analysis
We must find the internal emitter resistance $r_e$ before doing any AC math.

1. **Thevenin Base Voltage:** $V_{th} = 20\text{V} \times \frac{10\text{k}}{40\text{k} + 10\text{k}} = 4\text{ V}$
2. **Thevenin Base Resistance:** $R_{th} = 40\text{k} \parallel 10\text{k} = 8\text{ k}\Omega$
3. **Emitter Current:** $I_E = \frac{V_{th} - V_{BE}}{R_E + R_{th}/\beta} = \frac{4 - 0.7}{2000 + 8000/100} = \frac{3.3}{2080} \approx 1.587\text{ mA}$
4. **Dynamic Resistance:** $r_e = \frac{26\text{ mV}}{I_E} = \frac{26}{1.587} \approx 16.39\ \Omega$

### Step 1: Input Coupling Capacitor ($C_{in}$)
The resistance seen by $C_{in}$ is $R_{sig}$ plus the total input impedance of the amplifier.
The total input impedance is $R_1$ in parallel with $R_2$ in parallel with $\beta r_e$:

$$
Z_{in} = 40\text{k} \parallel 10\text{k} \parallel (100 \times 16.39) = 8000 \parallel 1639 \approx 1360\ \Omega
$$

Add $R_{sig}$:

$$
R_{eq(in)} = R_{sig} + Z_{in} = 0 + 1360 = 1360\ \Omega
$$

Calculate cutoff frequency:

$$
f_{L(in)} = \frac{1}{2\pi \times 1360 \times (10 \times 10^{-6})} \approx \mathbf{11.70\text{ Hz}}
$$

### Step 2: Output Coupling Capacitor ($C_{out}$)
The resistance seen by $C_{out}$ is the collector resistor plus the load resistor.

$$
R_{eq(out)} = R_C + R_L = 4\text{ k}\Omega + 2.2\text{ k}\Omega = 6.2\text{ k}\Omega
$$

Calculate cutoff frequency:

$$
f_{L(out)} = \frac{1}{2\pi \times 6200 \times (1 \times 10^{-6})} \approx \mathbf{25.67\text{ Hz}}
$$

### Step 3: Emitter Bypass Capacitor ($C_E$)
The resistance seen by $C_E$ is $R_E$ in parallel with the resistance looking back up into the emitter.
The resistance looking up is $r_e$ plus the reflected base resistance. The reflected resistance is $\frac{R_{sig} \parallel R_{th}}{\beta}$.
Here, $R_{sig} = 0$. The reflected term disappears completely. So looking up, we just see $r_e$.

$$
R_{eq(E)} = R_E \parallel r_e = 2000 \parallel 16.39 \approx 16.26\ \Omega
$$

Calculate cutoff frequency:

$$
f_{L(E)} = \frac{1}{2\pi \times 16.26 \times (20 \times 10^{-6})} \approx \mathbf{489.41\text{ Hz}}
$$

### Step 4: Overall Lower Cutoff Frequency
Once again, we pick the highest frequency.

$$
f_L \approx \max(11.70, 25.67, 489.41) = \boxed{ \mathbf{489.41\text{ Hz}} }
$$

## ⚡ Exam Tips

1. **Master the bypass capacitor formula.** This is where most students lose marks. 
   - For a JFET, the formula is $R_S \parallel (1/g_m)$.
   - For a BJT, the formula is $R_E \parallel (r_e + \frac{R_{sig} \parallel R_{th}}{\beta})$. Do not forget the reflected base resistance if $R_{sig}$ is greater than zero!
2. **Coupling capacitors use simple sums.** $C_{in}$ sees $R_{sig} + Z_{in}$. $C_{out}$ sees $R_C + R_L$. You just add them up.
3. **Pick the maximum value.** The final answer is the MAXIMUM of the three frequencies. Do not add them together.
