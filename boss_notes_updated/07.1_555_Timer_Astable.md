# 555 Timer: Internal Architecture and Astable Mode
> **Topic:** 555 Timer | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** 8/8 years (Guaranteed Question)

## Basic Knowledge: The Most Famous Chip in History

Before 1971, if you wanted a circuit to wait 5 seconds and then turn on a light, you had to build a complex mechanical relay system or use dozens of expensive discrete transistors. 

Then, the 555 timer integrated circuit (IC) was invented. It crammed 25 transistors, 2 diodes, and 15 resistors onto a single microscopic piece of silicon. It allowed engineers to create perfect, reliable delays and continuous clock pulses using just one cheap chip and a single resistor-capacitor pair. 

It is still the most popular integrated circuit ever manufactured.

---

## 1. Internal Architecture and Pins
*[Appeared in: 2024 Q7(a), 2022 Q4(a), 2019 Q7(a), 2018 Q7(a)]*

The 555 timer is a masterpiece of analog IC design. You must be able to draw its internal block diagram and explain the role of its pins on the exam.

### Key Internal Blocks
1. **The Voltage Divider:** It contains three perfectly matched $5\text{ k}\Omega$ resistors in series between the power supply ($V_{CC}$) and Ground. This network sets two rock-solid internal reference voltages: $1/3 V_{CC}$ and $2/3 V_{CC}$. This is exactly where the famous "555" name comes from!
2. **Two Comparators:** 
   - **Threshold Comparator:** This compares Pin 6 (Threshold) to the $2/3 V_{CC}$ reference node.
   - **Trigger Comparator:** This compares Pin 2 (Trigger) to the $1/3 V_{CC}$ reference node.
3. **RS Flip-Flop:** This digital memory block stores the current state (HIGH or LOW) based on the outputs of the two comparators.
4. **Discharge Transistor:** This is a heavy-duty NPN transistor driven by the inverted output of the Flip-Flop. When the timer output is LOW, this transistor turns ON and forcefully shorts Pin 7 (Discharge) directly to ground.

### Pin Functions
- **Pin 2 (Trigger):** When the voltage drops below $1/3 V_{CC}$, it sets the Flip-Flop. The Output immediately goes **HIGH**.
- **Pin 6 (Threshold):** When the voltage rises above $2/3 V_{CC}$, it resets the Flip-Flop. The Output immediately goes **LOW**.
- **Pin 7 (Discharge):** This pin shorts to ground whenever the output is LOW. It is used to drain capacitors.
- **Pin 5 (Control Voltage):** This allows you to externally override the internal $2/3 V_{CC}$ reference voltage.
- **Pin 3 (Output):** This is the main output. It is very powerful and can source or sink up to 200 mA of current.

## 2. Astable Multivibrator Mode
*[Appeared in: 2022 Q4(b), 2020 Q7(a)]*

In Astable mode, the 555 acts as a free-running oscillator. It generates a continuous square wave forever without needing any external triggers.

### The Circuit Wiring
We connect resistor $R_A$ from $V_{CC}$ to Pin 7. We connect resistor $R_B$ from Pin 7 to Pins 6 and 2. We connect capacitor $C$ from Pins 6 and 2 directly to ground.

### The Timing Cycle
1. **Charging Phase ($t_H$):** 
   - The output is HIGH. The internal discharge transistor is OFF.
   - Capacitor $C$ slowly charges from $1/3 V_{CC}$ up to $2/3 V_{CC}$.
   - It charges by pulling current through **both** $R_A$ and $R_B$.
   - **$t_H = 0.693 (R_A + R_B) C$**
2. **Discharging Phase ($t_L$):** 
   - The capacitor hits $2/3 V_{CC}$. The Threshold comparator fires. The output snaps LOW.
   - The discharge transistor turns ON. This violently shorts Pin 7 to ground.
   - The capacitor discharges from $2/3 V_{CC}$ down to $1/3 V_{CC}$.
   - It discharges through **only** $R_B$ directly into Pin 7.
   - **$t_L = 0.693 (R_B) C$**
3. **Total Period ($T$):**
   - $T = t_H + t_L = 0.693(R_A + 2R_B)C$
4. **Frequency ($f_o$):**
   - $f_o = 1/T = \frac{1.44}{(R_A + 2R_B)C}$

Because the capacitor always charges through $(R_A + R_B)$ but discharges through only $R_B$, **charging always takes longer than discharging**. Because of this physical reality, a standard 555 astable circuit *cannot* produce a perfectly symmetric 50 percent duty cycle. The duty cycle is always greater than 50 percent.

## 3. Worked Astable Design Problem
*[Appeared in: 2019 Q7(c)]*

**Problem Details:** Design a 555 astable oscillator with an output frequency $f_o = 3.5\text{ kHz}$ and a duty cycle $D = 60\%$.

**Step 1: Solve for the Resistor Relationship using Duty Cycle**

$$
D = \frac{t_H}{T} = \frac{R_A + R_B}{R_A + 2 R_B} = 0.60
$$

Cross-multiply:

$$
R_A + R_B = 0.60(R_A + 2 R_B)
$$

$$
R_A + R_B = 0.6 R_A + 1.2 R_B
$$

$$
0.4 R_A = 0.2 R_B \implies \mathbf{R_B = 2 R_A}
$$

**Step 2: Solve for RC Components using Frequency**

$$
f_o = \frac{1.44}{(R_A + 2 R_B) C} = 3500
$$

Substitute $R_B = 2R_A$ into the formula:

$$
3500 = \frac{1.44}{(R_A + 4 R_A) C} = \frac{1.44}{5 R_A C}
$$

$$
5 R_A C = \frac{1.44}{3500} \approx 4.114 \times 10^{-4}
$$

Let's pick a standard capacitor: **$C = 10\text{ nF}$** ($10^{-8}\text{ F}$).

$$
5 R_A (10^{-8}) = 4.114 \times 10^{-4}
$$

$$
R_A = \frac{4.114 \times 10^{-4}}{5 \times 10^{-8}} \approx 8228\ \Omega
$$

Choose standard values: **$R_A = 8.2\text{ k}\Omega$**, **$R_B = 16\text{ k}\Omega$**.

## 4. Astable Mode with Bypass Diode
*[Appeared in: 2018 Q7(b)]*

If you absolutely need a duty cycle of exactly 50 percent or lower, you must add a diode in parallel with $R_B$. Put the anode at Pin 7 and the cathode at Pin 6.

### How it alters the math:
1. **Charging Phase ($t_H$):** Current flows from $V_{CC}$ through $R_A$. The diode is forward-biased and creates a short-circuit around $R_B$. The capacitor now charges *only* through $R_A$. 

$$
t_H = 0.693 (R_A) C
$$

2. **Discharging Phase ($t_L$):** The capacitor tries to push current backward. The diode is reverse-biased and blocks the flow. The capacitor is forced to discharge through $R_B$ as normal.

$$
t_L = 0.693 (R_B) C
$$

If you carefully set $R_A$ perfectly equal to $R_B$, you get exactly a 50 percent duty cycle square wave!

### Worked Numerical
**Calculate the duty cycle of an astable circuit with a bypass diode across $R_B$, given $R_A = 1\text{ k}\Omega$ and $R_B = 10\text{ k}\Omega$.**

$$
D = \frac{t_H}{t_H + t_L} = \frac{R_A}{R_A + R_B} \times 100\%
$$

$$
D = \frac{1\text{k}}{1\text{k} + 10\text{k}} \times 100\% = \frac{1}{11} \times 100\% \approx \mathbf{9.09\%}
$$

## ⚡ Exam Tips
- **The Standard Formula.** The standard duty cycle formula WITHOUT a diode is $(R_A + R_B) / (R_A + 2R_B)$.
- **The Diode Formula.** The special duty cycle formula WITH a diode is simply $R_A / (R_A + R_B)$.
- **Memorize the blocks.** You must know how to draw the internal block diagram. Memorize these components: Three resistors, two comparators, one flip-flop, one transistor.
