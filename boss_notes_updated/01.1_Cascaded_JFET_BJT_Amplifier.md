# The Cascaded JFET-BJT Amplifier
> **Topic:** Multistage Amplifiers | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** 7/8 years
> **This is the single most important numerical problem in the entire course.**

## Basic Knowledge: What is a Cascaded Amplifier?

A cascaded amplifier connects two or more amplifier stages together. The output of the first stage connects directly to the input of the next stage. We do this because one transistor usually cannot provide enough voltage gain and input impedance on its own. 

In this circuit, we use a Junction Field Effect Transistor (JFET) for the first stage. We use a Bipolar Junction Transistor (BJT) for the second stage. 

Why use two different transistors? A JFET has a very high input impedance. This means it draws almost no current from the signal source. It will not load the source. But a JFET has low voltage gain. A BJT has very high voltage gain. But it has low input impedance. 

So, we combine them. The JFET stage takes the tiny input signal without loading the source. The BJT stage takes the JFET's output and multiplies it by a huge amount. The final result is an amplifier with both high input impedance and high voltage gain.

## Why This Circuit Matters

This specific circuit has appeared in 7 out of 8 final exams. It usually appears as Question 1. The component values are often exactly the same. You should practice this solution until you can do it quickly. It will guarantee you easy marks.

### Component Values to Learn

**Stage 1: JFET Common-Source**
- $R_G = 3.3\text{ M}\Omega$, $R_D = 2.4\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_S = 680\;\Omega$ (bypassed by $C_S$)
- JFET Parameters: $I_{DSS} = 10\text{ mA}$, $V_P = -4\text{ V}$

**Stage 2: BJT Common-Emitter**
- $R_{B1} = 15\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_{B2} = 4.7\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_C = 2.2\text{ k}\Omega$, $R_E = 1\text{ k}\Omega$ (bypassed by $C_E$)
- BJT Parameters: $\beta = 200$, $V_{BE} = 0.7\text{ V}$

**Power Supply:** $V_{DD} = V_{CC} = +20\text{ V}$

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: DC Bias of the JFET Stage

First, we analyze the DC conditions of the JFET. In DC analysis, all capacitors act as open circuits.

The JFET gate draws zero DC current. This means $I_G = 0$. Because no current flows through the gate resistor $R_G$, the voltage drop across it is zero. So, the gate voltage is $0\text{ V}$.

The current $I_D$ flows through the source resistor $R_S$. This creates a positive voltage at the source terminal. The gate is at $0\text{ V}$. The source is at a positive voltage. This makes the gate-source voltage ($V_{GS}$) negative. This is called self-bias. An n-channel JFET needs a negative $V_{GS}$ to operate correctly.

We calculate $V_{GS}$:

$$
V_{GS} = V_G - V_S = 0 - I_D R_S = -680 \, I_D
$$

Next, we use Shockley's equation. This equation relates the drain current to the gate-source voltage.

$$
I_D = I_{DSS} \left(1 - \frac{V_{GS}}{V_P}\right)^2
$$

We put our known values into the equation:

$$
I_D = 10\text{ mA} \left(1 - \frac{-680\,I_D}{-4}\right)^2
$$

$$
I_D = 0.01 \left(1 - 170\,I_D\right)^2
$$

We expand the squared term:

$$
I_D = 0.01 \left(1 - 340\,I_D + 28900\,I_D^2\right)
$$

$$
I_D = 0.01 - 3.4\,I_D + 289\,I_D^2
$$

We rearrange this into a standard quadratic equation:

$$
289\,I_D^2 - 4.4\,I_D + 0.01 = 0
$$

Now we use the quadratic formula to solve for $I_D$:

$$
I_D = \frac{4.4 \pm \sqrt{4.4^2 - 4(289)(0.01)}}{2(289)} = \frac{4.4 \pm \sqrt{19.36 - 11.56}}{578} = \frac{4.4 \pm 2.793}{578}
$$

This gives two possible solutions:
- $I_D \approx 12.4\text{ mA}$. We reject this. It is physically impossible because it is larger than $I_{DSS} = 10\text{ mA}$.
- $I_D \approx 2.78\text{ mA}$. We accept this correct value.

We use this current to find the resting operating point (Q-point):

$$
V_{GSQ} = -680 \times 2.78\text{ mA} \approx -1.89\text{ V}
$$

Finally, we calculate the transconductance $g_m$. Transconductance tells us how much the drain current changes when the gate voltage changes. It acts as the gain factor for the JFET.

$$
g_m = \frac{2\,I_{DSS}}{|V_P|}\left(1 - \frac{V_{GSQ}}{V_P}\right) = \frac{2 \times 10\text{ mA}}{4}\left(1 - \frac{-1.89}{-4}\right) = 5\text{ mS} \times 0.5275 \approx 2.64\text{ mS}
$$

---

### Step 2: DC Bias of the BJT Stage

Next, we move to the BJT stage. We use Thevenin's theorem to simplify the voltage divider at the base. We replace resistors $R_{B1}$ and $R_{B2}$ with a single voltage source and a single resistor.

The Thevenin voltage is:

$$
V_{th} = V_{CC} \times \frac{R_{B2}}{R_{B1} + R_{B2}} = 20\text{ V} \times \frac{4.7\text{k}}{15\text{k} + 4.7\text{k}} = 20 \times \frac{4.7}{19.7} \approx 4.77\text{ V}
$$

The Thevenin resistance is the parallel combination of $R_{B1}$ and $R_{B2}$:

$$
R_{th} = R_{B1} \| R_{B2} = \frac{15\text{k} \times 4.7\text{k}}{15\text{k} + 4.7\text{k}} = 3.58\text{ k}\Omega
$$

We apply Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) around the base-emitter loop. We start at $V_{th}$, go through $R_{th}$, the base-emitter junction, and $R_E$ to ground:

$$
V_{th} - I_B R_{th} - V_{BE} - I_E R_E = 0
$$

We know that emitter current $I_E$ is roughly equal to $\beta \times I_B$ because $\beta$ is large.

$$
4.77 - I_E \left(\frac{3.58\text{k}}{200}\right) - 0.7 - I_E (1\text{k}) = 0
$$

$$
4.07 = I_E \times 1.018\text{k} \quad \Rightarrow \quad I_{EQ} \approx 4.0\text{ mA}
$$

Now we find the dynamic emitter resistance $r_e$. This is the internal resistance of the BJT. It controls the small-signal gain.

$$
r_e = \frac{26\text{ mV}}{I_{EQ}} = \frac{26}{4.0} = 6.5\;\Omega
$$

---

### Step 3: AC Analysis and Gain Calculation

Now we perform AC analysis. We treat all DC supplies as ground. We treat all capacitors as short circuits.

**Input Impedance of Stage 2 ($Z_{i2}$):**

First, we find the input impedance of the BJT stage. The base sees the Thevenin resistance in parallel with the transistor's internal resistance multiplied by $\beta$.

$$
Z_{i2} = R_{th} \| \beta r_e = 3.58\text{k} \| (200 \times 6.5) = 3.58\text{k} \| 1.3\text{k} \approx 953\;\Omega
$$

This value is very important. This impedance acts as the load for Stage 1. It pulls down the gain of the JFET stage. This is called the loading effect.

**Stage 1 Voltage Gain ($A_{v1}$):**

The JFET stage is a common-source amplifier. Its gain is $-g_m$ times the total load it sees. The load is $R_D$ in parallel with $Z_{i2}$.

$$
A_{v1} = -g_m (R_D \| Z_{i2}) = -2.64\text{ mS} \times (2.4\text{k} \| 953) = -2.64\text{ mS} \times 682.3 \approx -1.8
$$

The negative sign means the signal flips upside down. This is a 180-degree phase shift.

**Stage 2 Voltage Gain ($A_{v2}$):**

The BJT stage is a common-emitter amplifier. The emitter capacitor $C_E$ bypasses the emitter resistor $R_E$. The gain equation is:

$$
A_{v2} = -\frac{R_C}{r_e} = -\frac{2.2\text{k}}{6.5} \approx -338.5
$$

This stage also flips the signal upside down.

**Overall Voltage Gain ($A_v$):**

We multiply the gains of both stages to find the total gain.

$$
A_v = A_{v1} \times A_{v2} = (-1.8) \times (-338.5) \approx \boxed{609.3}
$$

Both negative signs cancel out. The final output has the same phase as the input signal.

---

### Step 4: Overall Impedances

**Overall Input Impedance ($Z_i$):**

We look into the JFET gate to find the input impedance. The gate draws zero current. The input impedance is simply the gate resistor.

$$
Z_i = R_G = 3.3\text{ M}\Omega
$$

This huge value means the amplifier barely takes any current from the signal source.

**Overall Output Impedance ($Z_o$):**

We look back into the BJT collector. The output impedance is just the collector resistor.

$$
Z_o = R_C = 2.2\text{ k}\Omega
$$

---

### Step 5: Output Waveform

If the input signal is $v_i(t) = 10\sin(\omega t)\text{ mV}$:

$$
v_o(t) = A_v \times v_i(t) = 609.3 \times 10\sin(\omega t)\text{ mV} \approx 6.09\sin(\omega t)\text{ V}
$$

- The output stays in-phase with the input.
- The signal grows from 10 mV to about 6.09 V.

## ⚡ Exam Tips and Common Mistakes

1. **Reject the larger $I_D$ root.** If $I_D$ is larger than $I_{DSS}$, it is physically impossible. You must pick the smaller root.
2. **Account for the loading effect.** Stage 1 gain is not just $-g_m R_D$. You must include Stage 2 input impedance. Use $A_{v1} = -g_m(R_D \| Z_{i2})$. Students lose marks here often.
3. **Check your phases.** Two inversions make an in-phase output. When you sketch the output wave, make sure it matches the input phase.
4. **$Z_i$ is just $R_G$.** Do not overcomplicate this part. The JFET gate draws zero current. The input impedance is simply the gate resistor.
