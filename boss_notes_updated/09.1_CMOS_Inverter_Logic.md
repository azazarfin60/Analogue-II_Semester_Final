# CMOS Logic Gates
> **Topic:** CMOS Logic | **Priority:** 🔴 CRITICAL | **Exam Frequency:** 5/8 years

## Basic Knowledge: Transistors as Switches

In analog circuits, we use transistors as amplifiers. We carefully bias them into their linear region so they can multiply small signals.

In digital circuits, we use transistors purely as electronic switches. They are either fully ON or fully OFF. 
- When a transistor is fully ON, it acts like a closed wire. It connects the output to the power supply (Logic 1) or to ground (Logic 0). 
- When a transistor is fully OFF, it acts like an open switch. It disconnects the output.

By arranging multiple transistor switches in series and parallel, we can build physical circuits that execute mathematical logic (like AND, OR, and NOT). We call these Logic Gates.

---

## 1. CMOS Technology Principles
*[Appeared in: 2023 Q3(c), 2021 Q8b]*

CMOS stands for **Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor**. This technology mathematically pairs complementary p-channel (PMOS) and n-channel (NMOS) enhancement-mode MOSFETs. 

It is the dominant technology for all modern digital integrated circuits (including all computer processors). It dominates because of its massive structural advantages:

1. **Ultra-Low Static Power Dissipation:** Under steady-state conditions, either the pull-up network (PMOS) or the pull-down network (NMOS) is entirely OFF. Because they are in series, no direct DC path ever exists between the power supply ($V_{DD}$) and Ground. Static power dissipation is essentially zero (in the nanowatt range). Current is only drawn for a tiny microsecond during active logic transitions.
2. **Symmetrical Rail-to-Rail Swings:** The HIGH logic state is pulled directly up to $+V_{DD}$ via an ON PMOS transistor. The LOW logic state is pulled directly down to Ground via an ON NMOS transistor. The signal does not suffer annoying voltage drops like it does in BJT logic.
3. **High Input Impedance:** The MOS gate is insulated by a thin layer of silicon dioxide glass ($SiO_2$). Because of this glass, CMOS inputs draw absolutely zero DC gate leakage current.
4. **High Packing Density:** MOSFETs are physically microscopic compared to BJTs. They require no internal isolation resistors. This allows engineers to fabricate billions of logic gates on a single tiny silicon CPU die.

## 2. The CMOS Inverter (NOT Gate)
*[Appeared in: 2017 Q1(c)]*

This is the fundamental building block of all digital logic.
* **Pull-up:** One PMOS transistor. The Source connects to $V_{DD}$. The Drain connects to the Output.
* **Pull-down:** One NMOS transistor. The Source connects to Ground. The Drain connects to the Output.
* **Inputs:** The two gates tie together to form the single input $V_{in}$.

```
                      +VDD
                       |
                     [ PMOS ] 
                       |
        Vin o----------+---> Vout
                       |
                     [ NMOS ] 
                       |
                      Gnd
```

### Operation:
- **Input HIGH ($V_{DD}$):** The NMOS turns ON because $V_{GS} = V_{DD}$. The PMOS turns OFF because $V_{GS} = 0$. The output is physically pulled down to **Ground (LOW)**.
- **Input LOW ($0\text{V}$):** The NMOS turns OFF because $V_{GS} = 0$. The PMOS turns ON because $V_{GS} = -V_{DD}$. The output is physically pulled up to **$V_{DD}$ (HIGH)**.

## 3. Designing 2-Input Gates

The golden rule of CMOS design is simple:
- **PMOS (Pull-up network):** These turn ON when the input is LOW. Connect them in **Parallel** for NAND logic. Connect them in **Series** for NOR logic.
- **NMOS (Pull-down network):** These turn ON when the input is HIGH. Connect them in **Series** for NAND logic. Connect them in **Parallel** for NOR logic.

*(Notice they are always the exact topological opposite of each other!)*

### 3.1 2-Input NAND Gate
*[Appeared in: 2022 Q4(a), 2021 Q8(c)]*
- **PMOS:** Parallel.
- **NMOS:** Series.
If *both* inputs are HIGH, both series NMOS turn ON. This pulls the output LOW. If *any* input is LOW, the series path breaks. A parallel PMOS turns ON instead. This pulls the output HIGH.

### 3.2 2-Input NOR Gate
*[Appeared in: 2021 Q8(c)]*
- **PMOS:** Series.
- **NMOS:** Parallel.
If *both* inputs are LOW, both series PMOS turn ON. This pulls the output HIGH. If *any* input is HIGH, the series path breaks. A parallel NMOS turns ON instead. This pulls the output LOW.

## 4. Designing a 3-Input NAND Gate
*[Appeared in: 2022 Q1(c), 2017 Q2(a)]*

This is the most common 6-mark drawing and truth-table question.
To implement the logic equation $V_{out} = \overline{A \cdot B \cdot C}$:
- **Pull-up Network:** Place three PMOS transistors ($Q_{P1}, Q_{P2}, Q_{P3}$) perfectly in **parallel** between $+V_{DD}$ and the output node.
- **Pull-down Network:** Place three NMOS transistors ($Q_{N1}, Q_{N2}, Q_{N3}$) perfectly in **series** between the output node and Ground.

```
                           +VDD
                 +-----+-----+-----+
                 |     |     |     |
                Qp1   Qp2   Qp3    |   (Parallel PMOS Pull-up)
                 |     |     |     |
                 +-----+-----+-----+---> Vout
                       |
                      Qn1
                       |
                      Qn2              (Series NMOS Pull-down)
                       |
                      Qn3
                       |
                      Gnd
```

### Truth Table Analysis

| Input A | Input B | Input C | $Q_{P1}$ | $Q_{P2}$ | $Q_{P3}$ | $Q_{N1}$ | $Q_{N2}$ | $Q_{N3}$ | Output $V_{out}$ |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **0** | **0** | **0** | ON | ON | ON | OFF | OFF | OFF | **1** |
| **0** | **0** | **1** | ON | ON | OFF | OFF | OFF | ON | **1** |
| **0** | **1** | **0** | ON | OFF | ON | OFF | ON | OFF | **1** |
| **0** | **1** | **1** | ON | OFF | OFF | OFF | ON | ON | **1** |
| **1** | **0** | **0** | OFF | ON | ON | ON | OFF | OFF | **1** |
| **1** | **0** | **1** | OFF | ON | OFF | ON | OFF | ON | **1** |
| **1** | **1** | **0** | OFF | OFF | ON | ON | ON | OFF | **1** |
| **1** | **1** | **1** | OFF | OFF | OFF | ON | ON | ON | **0** |

**Verification:** Look at the bottom row. The output is LOW (0) *only* when all three inputs are HIGH (1). This is because all three series NMOS transistors turn ON simultaneously. In all other cases, the series path breaks and at least one PMOS activates to pull the output HIGH (1). This perfectly matches standard NAND logic.

## ⚡ Exam Tips
- **The big keyword.** The phrase **"Ultra-Low Static Power Dissipation"** is the single most important feature of CMOS technology. Never omit it.
- **Drawing clearly.** In drawing questions, clearly label the PMOS transistors with a small circle (bubble) at the gate terminal. This proves you know how to distinguish them from the NMOS transistors.
- **Start with transistors.** When generating a truth table for complex CMOS gates, fill out the internal transistor states first (0 turns PMOS ON, 1 turns NMOS ON). The final output logic will then follow naturally without any guessing.
