# Feedback Pair (Complementary Darlington)
> **Topic:** Multistage Amplifiers | **Priority:** 🟢 OCCASIONAL | **Exam Frequency:** 2/8 years

## Basic Knowledge: Mixing Transistor Types

The Darlington pair connects two identical transistors together. It uses two NPN transistors or two PNP transistors. But what happens if we mix them? 

A Feedback Pair uses two different types of transistors. It connects a PNP transistor to an NPN transistor. We call these "complementary" transistors. 

This mix changes the way current flows through the circuit. The collector of the second transistor (NPN) connects back to the emitter of the first transistor (PNP). This creates a loop. We call this a feedback loop. 

This feedback loop makes the circuit very stable. Like the Darlington pair, it still provides massive current gain. The total gain is the product of both individual gains. But it only has one base-emitter voltage drop at the input instead of two. This makes the math and the design slightly different.

## The Circuit Diagram

```
             V_CC
              |
         +----+-------+
         |            |
        [R_B]        [R_C]
         |            |
    C_s  |            +-----+----- V_o
   --||--+            |     |
         |        Q1 (PNP)  |
         |         /|       |
         +----- B-| Q1      |
                   \|        |
                    +--C1----+-- B(Q2)
                    |E1      |
                    |     Q2 (NPN)
                    |      |\
                    |   C2-| Q2
                    |      |/
                   ===      +--E2
                   GND      |
                           ===
                           GND
```

**Key Connections:**
- Q1 is a PNP transistor. Its emitter connects to the top voltage supply ($V_{CC}$). Its collector points down.
- Q2 is an NPN transistor. Its collector feeds back up to the same resistor path ($R_C$).
- The collector of Q1 connects directly to the base of Q2.
- The collector current of Q1 becomes the base current of Q2. So, $I_{B2} = I_{C1}$.

---

## Step-by-Step DC Analysis

The current math looks like the Darlington math. But the physical current path is different because Q1 is a PNP transistor.

**Step 1: Find Q1 Collector Current**

The collector current of Q1 depends on its base current:

$$
I_{C1} = \beta_1 I_{B1}
$$

**Step 2: Connect to Q2**

This current flows straight into the base of Q2:

$$
I_{B2} = I_{C1} = \beta_1 I_{B1}
$$

**Step 3: Find Q2 Collector Current**

Q2 amplifies this current again:

$$
I_{C2} = \beta_2 I_{B2} = \beta_1 \beta_2 I_{B1}
$$

**Step 4: Find Total Circuit Current**

The total current flowing through the top resistor $R_C$ combines two currents. It includes the emitter current of Q1 and the collector current of Q2.

$$
I_C = I_{E1} + I_{C2} = (\beta_1 + 1)I_{B1} + \beta_1\beta_2 I_{B1}
$$

We group the terms together:

$$
I_C = I_{B1}(\beta_1 + 1 + \beta_1\beta_2)
$$

The product term $\beta_1\beta_2$ is much bigger than $\beta_1 + 1$. We can ignore the small terms. The total current simplifies to:

$$
I_C \approx \beta_1\beta_2 I_{B1}
$$

**Step 5: Find the Input Base Current ($I_{B1}$)**

We use Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) around the outer loop to find $I_{B1}$:

$$
V_{CC} - I_C R_C - V_{BE1} - I_{B1} R_B = 0
$$

We replace $I_C$ with our simplified formula:

$$
V_{CC} - (\beta_1\beta_2 I_{B1}) R_C - V_{BE1} - I_{B1} R_B = 0
$$

We rearrange the formula to solve for $I_{B1}$:

$$
I_{B1} = \frac{V_{CC} - V_{BE1}}{R_B + \beta_1\beta_2 R_C}
$$

**Step 6: Find Key Voltages**

We can find the important DC voltages in the circuit:

$$
V_{B1} = I_{B1} \cdot R_B
$$

$$
V_{C1} = V_{BE2} = 0.7\text{ V}
$$

$$
V_{C2} = V_{CC} - I_C R_C
$$

## Key Differences: Darlington vs. Feedback Pair

| Feature | Darlington Pair | Feedback Pair |
|---|---|---|
| **Transistor Types** | Same (NPN-NPN or PNP-PNP) | Different (PNP-NPN) |
| **Input $V_{BE}$ Drop** | $2 \times V_{BE} = 1.4\text{ V}$ | Only $1 \times V_{BE} = 0.7\text{ V}$ |
| **Total Current Gain** | $\beta_D \approx \beta_1\beta_2$ | $\approx \beta_1\beta_2$ (same result) |
| **$I_{B1}$ Formula** | $\frac{V_{CC} - 2V_{BE}}{R_B + \beta_D R_E}$ | $\frac{V_{CC} - V_{BE}}{R_B + \beta_1\beta_2 R_C}$ |
| **Stability** | Normal | High (due to feedback loop) |

## ⚡ Exam Tips

- Notice the $R_C$ in the feedback pair formula. The Darlington formula uses $R_E$ in the denominator. This happens because the total current flows through $R_C$, not a separate emitter resistor.
- Remember there is only ONE $V_{BE}$ drop in the $I_{B1}$ formula. The Darlington pair has two $V_{BE}$ drops. Do not mix this up.
